Abstract

We describe athenea, a USD and Hydra renderer in which every step that touches scene data runs as a GPU compute kernel, and the pipeline built on it that turns production USD meshes into 3D Gaussian splats that keep their light. A mesh is converted on the device into anisotropic Gaussians, one per cell of a camera-derived lattice, with coverage-correct opacity, edges held where a surface stops or folds, layered OpenPBR materials, glass classified as solid or thin wall, and roughness widened by the spread of normals under each Gaussian. The scene’s own path tracer then bakes a transfer into every Gaussian: degree-3 spherical harmonics of direct and indirect light, a reflected field, and 256 bits of open directions. At draw time these relight the cloud under any sky and any light, with sharp reflections turned per pixel by a stored shape operator. Rigged assets keep their skeleton, with a Jacobian that includes the weights’ gradients, zonal transfer lobes that turn with a limb and per-part visibility fields. Per-part merge trees with a monotone, bounded screen error, back-face and occlusion culls, and pages ordered by error give levels of detail. Clustered PCA, vector-quantised visibility and a 40-byte compact record carry the whole Chevrolet Corvette C7 to a WebGPU player on an iPhone. Throughout, the reference is a path trace of the source mesh. Doubling the bake resolution from 640 to 1280 px raised the car’s PSNR from 16.55 to 17.88 dB on its crop and from 22.18 to 23.90 dB on the frame. The level of detail costs about 0.1 dB. On the phone, the car data stayed at 31–44 MB while moving. A 5120 px close-up level of 74.5 million leaves in 24 trees was built for close views.

Keywords: 3D Gaussian splatting; USD; Hydra; precomputed radiance transfer; spherical harmonics; mesh to Gaussians; level of detail; clustered PCA; WebGPU; mobile rendering; path tracing; GPU-only pipelines.

1. Introduction

1.1 The problem

Production 3D assets are authored as meshes: polygons, layered physically based materials, textures, lights and, for characters, skeletons, all composed in USD. They are rendered faithfully by a path tracer, which is too slow for a phone, and interactively by a rasteriser, which needs the asset’s full geometry and texture set resident and still approximates its light transport. 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) offers a third route: a cloud of anisotropic Gaussians, sorted and blended in screen space, draws photographic detail at interactive rates on modest hardware. A Gaussian cloud, however, is normally trained from photographs of a real scene. It holds the light of that capture, it cannot be relit, and it has no material, no skeleton and no relation to an authored asset.

This paper addresses the converse problem. Given a photoreal USD asset (the case followed throughout is a Chevrolet Corvette C7 of 192 meshes with OpenPBR car paint, chrome, glass, rubber and lamps), produce a Gaussian representation that

  1. is faithful to a path trace of the source mesh, judged image against image and region by region;
  2. relights: it answers any sky and any light, not only the illumination it was made under;
  3. keeps a rig where the asset has one, so that an animated asset still moves;
  4. plays back on a phone inside a web page, within a fixed memory budget, close up as well as far away.

Neither of the obvious routes meets all four. Training a cloud from path-traced views of the asset gives a fixed illumination and spends hours of optimisation per asset. Rasterising the mesh on the phone is limited by memory and by the shading a mobile GPU can afford per pixel. The route taken here converts the mesh directly into Gaussians, bakes the asset’s light transport into each Gaussian with the renderer’s own path tracer, and then reduces, compresses and streams the result.

1.3 Contributions

The contributions described in this paper are the following.

  1. A GPU-only USD renderer (athenea, Section 2) that draws meshes, points and Gaussian splats through Hydra, with a tile rasteriser and a Gaussian ray tracer that share one Gaussian layout, a MaterialX-to-Slang material compiler checked against MaterialX itself, a path tracer with multiple importance sampling used as ground truth, and a verification discipline in which every image comparison is itself a GPU kernel.
  2. A mesh-to-Gaussian conversion (mesh2splat, Section 3), run as an AOFX effect on the device, with cells derived from a camera’s pixel footprint, opacity that reproduces a surface’s coverage, hard edges and closed shells found on the device, Toksvig widening of roughness by the normals’ spread, layered OpenPBR capture, and automatic glass classification.
  3. A relightable transfer baked into each Gaussian (TX, Section 4): degree-3 spherical harmonics of direct and indirect transfer, a reflected field normalised over closed directions and coupled to the sky at draw time, and 256 bits of open directions read as cones by each reflection lobe; per-pixel sharp lobes driven by a stored shape operator, with the bake’s widening undone and a tube distinguished from a fold.
  4. Skinned Gaussian clouds (Section 5) that carry the rig rather than the frames: a skinning kernel using the full Jacobian including the weights’ gradients, zonal transfer lobes that turn with the Gaussian, per-part visibility fields read as a product of table lookups, and decimation that keeps the rig.
  5. Per-part merge trees with a bounded error cut (Section 6): pair merging under guards that respect parts and materials, a least-squares coverage opacity for merges, an error made monotone with covering spheres so that one parallel test selects the cut, back-face culling of closed shells, a conservative shared occlusion codebook, and pages ordered by the parent’s error.
  6. A compression and streaming path to a phone (Section 7): a .athc v3 container with byte-plane encodings, clustered PCA of the transfer kept compressed on the GPU, vector-quantised open-direction bits, a 40-byte compact record, quantised traversal records of 5–11 bytes, and a frame-time and still-frame policy that keeps the car’s data within its budget.
  7. A measured account (Section 8) of what each step buys against the path trace, on one production asset, from the bake to a real iPhone.

1.5 Related work

Gaussian splatting. Kerbl et al. (2023) introduced 3D Gaussian splatting: a scene as anisotropic Gaussians with spherical-harmonic colour, optimised from photographs and drawn by a tile-based, depth-sorted rasteriser. Its projection is the EWA splatting of Zwicker et al. (2001, 2002), a local affine approximation of the perspective map. Mip-Splatting (Yu et al., 2024) adds a 3D smoothing filter and a 2D screen filter against aliasing; athenea’s 0.3 px² dilation with its opacity compensation is of that family (§2.5.1). 2D Gaussian splatting (Huang et al., 2024) flattens each Gaussian to an oriented disc evaluated by ray–plane intersection, in the line of surfels (Pfister et al., 2000); athenea’s surfels follow it (§2.5.2). 3D Gaussian ray tracing (Moënne-Loccoz et al., 2024) traces particles through bounding proxies in a BVH, which athenea’s hardware route follows (§2.6).

From meshes to Gaussians. Electronic Arts’ mesh2splat converts a mesh into Gaussians by rasterising each triangle in its dominant axis plane, one Gaussian per covered texel; athenea’s conversion starts from it (§3.1). Mesh2GS (arXiv 2606.21898) derives a Gaussian’s size from a camera’s pixel footprint, which athenea’s --cell-from-camera follows (§3.3).

Precomputed light transport. Precomputed radiance transfer (Sloan, Kautz and Snyder, 2002) stores, per point, the projection of visibility times the cosine onto spherical harmonics, so that relighting is a dot product with the environment’s coefficients; Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan (2001) showed that nine coefficients represent irradiance well. Clustered PCA (Sloan et al., 2003) compresses such transfers by clustering and a per-cluster principal basis, which athenea uses both for the file and on the phone’s GPU (§7.2). Zonal harmonics, which rotate by rotating an axis, are fitted after Sloan (2008) (§4.4.5, §5.7). Filtered importance sampling (Křivánek and Colbert, 2008) prefilters the environment (§4.3), and Lagarde and de Rousiers (2014) give the specular occlusion fit that the first transfer used (§4.6.1).

Specular antialiasing. Toksvig (2005) widens a microfacet distribution by the variance of the normals it averages; athenea applies the same idea at conversion, to the spread of normals under one Gaussian (§3.7, §4.9).

Levels of detail for Gaussians. Octree-GS (Ren et al., 2024) organises anchors in an octree selected by view distance. Hierarchical 3D Gaussians (Kerbl et al., 2024) build a merge hierarchy from Gaussian moments and cut it by screen size; athenea’s original level of detail follows its moments (§6.1). NanoGS (Xiong et al., 2026) merges mutual nearest neighbours pairwise, which athenea’s per-part trees run on the device (§6.2). The cost uses Runnalls’ (2007) bound for Gaussian mixture reduction, and the error-band pages follow Nanite’s (Karis et al., 2021) use of a monotone error for a parallel cut (§6.3, §6.8).

Engines and formats. The engine is built on OpenUSD and Hydra, MaterialX and OpenPBR materials, Slang shaders compiled through slang-rhi to Metal, Vulkan and CUDA, Intel Open Image Denoise and OpenColorIO. The player is a fork of Spark with a WebGPU backend.

1.6 Conventions

Unless stated otherwise, measurements were taken on an Apple M5 Pro under Metal. Images are compared on the GPU (§2.15). PSNR is in dB on display-encoded images; relMSE is relative mean squared error on linear values. The path trace (PT) of the Corvette is the mesh path-traced from /World/Camera at 1920 × 1080 under the autoshop_01_4k sky. Car crop is PSNR on the 900 × 420 crop of the car, frame on the whole frame; a class ratio is a region’s mean over the path trace’s. The validate-corvette harness of the player measures other regions (car, door, sill, bumper, hood), which are not the car crop (§7.12.3). Test identifiers (T15–T75) are those of the pieces work’s log, and commit hashes are those of the repository named (Appendix A). The words Gaussian and splat are used interchangeably; a leaf is a baked Gaussian, a merge a node of a merge tree, and a cut the set of nodes drawn for one view. Transfer is the baked light transport (§4), TX its current form. Figures named in the sections as file paths are listed in Appendix A.3.

2. The athenea engine and renderer

athenea is a USD renderer built so that every step that touches scene data runs on the GPU. It draws three kinds of primitive: meshes, with MaterialX materials and UsdLux lights; points; and 3D Gaussian splats. It offers two techniques, an interactive raster and a path tracer that serves as ground truth. Hydra feeds both through the hdAthenea render delegate. This section covers the engine’s architecture, the algorithms each subsystem runs, how correctness is established without a CPU oracle, and what has been measured. Unless stated otherwise, every number is quoted from docs/decisions.md, with its unit and conditions, and was measured on an Apple M5 Pro under Metal.

2.1 Design rules

The codebase holds to a small number of rules. Each one is enforced by review and, where possible, by a test.

  1. No CPU arithmetic on data. athenea has no CPU reference renderer, no CPU fallback and no CPU test oracle. The host reads files, parses headers, decompresses, and keeps the books: counts, slots and queues. Decoding, sorting, merging, culling, mip generation, light power prefixes and image comparison are all compute kernels. Where a step that broke this rule turned up, it was moved to the device and the move was recorded. Examples are per-element interleaving of ParticleField arrays, half-to-float conversion, per-pixel conversion of Hydra render buffers, and each light’s cumulative share of the frame’s power (light_prefix.slang). The one documented exception is Dear ImGui’s tessellation, which processes viewer chrome and no scene data.
  2. Binding by name. Every shader parameter is bound through reflection (cursor["name"].setBinding(...), ComputeKernel::dispatch) and never by slot. The rule comes from slot-counting bugs met in the compositor that preceded this engine.
  3. Errors as values. Errors are reported as Result<T> with ATHENEA_TRY, and nothing throws. OS calls live only in modules/core/Platform, which is also where a Windows port would start.
  4. One Slang, one slang-rhi, one TBB per process. OpenUSD, OIDN and the engine share one TBB, and the single_tbb test checks the count. For the same reason OIDN is built from source: its release binaries ship a second libtbb.12.
  5. GPU devices only, everywhere. OIDN is built for GPU devices only, because a denoiser that falls back to the CPU is a CPU fallback. A test whose capability is missing on a device skips and names the missing capability.

The backends are those of slang-rhi: Metal (macOS, iOS), Vulkan and CUDA (Linux, NVIDIA L4). All kernels are written in Slang.

2.3 GPU foundations

Device and kernels. gpu::Device wraps a slang-rhi device. ComputeKernel is one compute entry point compiled as a pipeline. It is dispatched in threads, not groups: the group count is rounded up on every backend and each kernel does its own bounds check. RasterKernel is a vertex and fragment pipeline that pulls its data from StructuredBuffers, with no vertex buffers or input layouts. RayTracingKernel covers OptiX and Vulkan RT pipelines. On Metal, slang-rhi offers no ray tracing pipelines, so tracing uses inline RayQuery inside compute kernels.

ShaderLibrary. The library loads Slang modules. A module may declare link-time constants (extern static const uint kName;) and is then linked against a generated exports module, which yields one program per distinct set of values. It also compiles modules that exist only as generated source, such as materials and the colour functions; loading an existing name with different source is refused. A persistent DiskShaderCache writes one file per compiled program, keyed by slang-rhi’s hash of the linked program. With a warm cache the whole test suite went from 89 s to 26 s.

Parallel primitives. PrefixSum and RadixSort are the only sorting and scanning in the engine. The tiled radix route (radix_block.slang) is a reduce-then-scan form of the stable LSD pass. A group of 256 threads counts a tile of 1024 keys in group memory, a digit-major prefix sum places each tile’s share of each digit, and the group sorts its tile with eight one-bit splits. It sorts 10 M 32-bit pairs in 12.4 ms, against 39.9 ms for the chunked passes it replaced from 65 536 elements up. It uses no wave operations and no device atomics.

Mips. slang-rhi does not generate mip chains, so MipGenerator is a kernel. Each texel of a level is the area-weighted mean of the texels above it. An odd edge 2n+12n+1 folds into nn with weights (n−x,n,x+1)/(2n+1)(n-x,\,n,\,x+1)/(2n+1), so every source texel gives exactly n/(2n+1)n/(2n+1) of itself to the level below and the chain preserves level 0’s mean. On sizes 64², 37×23, 1×9 and 128×5, the means agree to 2·10⁻⁶.

Memory as a first-class error. A Metal command buffer that runs out of memory used to assert inside slang-rhi on Metal’s completion thread, which ended the process. A patch now records the error on the queue (SLANG_E_OUT_OF_MEMORY) and returns it from the next submit() or waitOnHost(). Device::admit refuses any allocation that would exceed the budget: ATHENEA_GPU_BUDGET, or else Metal’s recommendedMaxWorkingSetSize, which is 18 186 MiB on the M5 Pro. An allocation must also fit the free physical memory less 1.5 GiB. On unified memory, four jobs that were each under Metal’s figure together swapped the machine until the window server missed its watchdog. When a frame runs out of memory, Engine::relieveMemory steps down: it drops splat shadows first, then moves up to four LOD levels coarser, and finally reopens streams at half their budget.

2.4 The Gaussian representation

Every renderer, the loaders and the LOD share one GPU layout, GpuSplats. Each splat takes 32 bytes before its harmonics, defined in common/packing.slang:

Field Encoding Reason
position, opacity 4 × float32 fp16 positions would step two pixels at 4K close up
rotation smallest-three quaternion, 3 × 10 bits + dropped index within a tenth of a degree
scale f16 of log⁡s\log s per axis 0.1 % relative precision at every size
base colour 3 × f16 may leave [0, 1]
SH rest shWords × u32, f16 per coefficient signed
normal (optional) octahedral 2 × 16 bits shading normal kept apart from the frame
emission (optional) RGB9E5 radiance the surface emits

CloudLoader reads PLY, SPZ, SOG, .splat and USD ParticleField prims. It uploads the raw records and decodes them on the GPU. For SOG, the host’s only work is to unzip the archive and decode the WebP images.

Linear light. Every gaussian is blended in linear Rec.709, and sRGB appears only where an image is displayed. A capture keeps the sRGB harmonics it was trained with: each projection evaluates them, clamps at zero and converts to linear (cloudLight) before blending. A cloud written by athenea declares that it holds light (primvars:athenea:splat:linear) and is used as stored. On two half-opaque overlapping discs, the rasteriser, the reference and the ray tracer each match the analytic over-operator at p99 0. Under the previous encoded blend, all three read p99 26.

2.5 The tile rasteriser

render::TileRasterizer is the interactive route for splats. It is a sort-based, deterministic tile rasteriser in the 3DGS family. Seven stages run on the device:

Stage Work
project per splat: covariance, conic, colour, 24-bit depth key, footprint, exact count of tiles touched
sort splats by depth (RadixSort)
gather tiles-touched counts in depth order, prefix sum
emit one (tile, splat) pair per touched tile, in depth order
sort pairs by tile, stably, on tile bits only
ranges start and end of each tile’s pairs
blend per pixel: its tile’s pairs, front to back

The engine sorts splats by depth once (N 32-bit keys) and then sorts pairs stably by tile (P keys of about 15 bits). This gives every tile its splats in depth order, with ties broken by splat index, exactly as a single 64-bit (tile, depth) sort would. The construction is deterministic. Tiles are 16 × 16 pixels.

2.5.1 Projection

A splat with rotation RR and scales S=diag(s)S=\mathrm{diag}(s) has the 3D covariance Σ=RSS⊤R⊤\Sigma = R S S^\top R^\top, built in the cloud’s own space. With WW the linear part of object-to-view and JJ the Jacobian of the perspective map at the view-space centre tt, the screen covariance is the EWA local affine approximation

Σ2D=(JWM)(JWM)⊤,M=RS,J=(fx/tz0−fxtx/tz20fy/tz−fyty/tz2).\Sigma_{2D} = (J W M)(J W M)^\top,\qquad M = R S,\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix} f_x/t_z & 0 & -f_x t_x/t_z^2\\ 0 & f_y/t_z & -f_y t_y/t_z^2\end{pmatrix}.

Before JJ is built, tx/tzt_x/t_z and ty/tzt_y/t_z are clamped to the frustum widened to [−0.15,1.15][-0.15, 1.15] of the image. A splat far off screen would otherwise smear across the whole frame, and 3DGS clamps in the same way. Orthographic cameras use J=diag(fx,fy)J=\mathrm{diag}(f_x,f_y).

Shutter and lens, as convolutions. A gaussian smeared along a straight screen path dd under a uniform shutter is that gaussian convolved with a segment. Matching second moments turns this into a rank-one update. A thin lens convolves with the disc of the circle of confusion:

Σ′=Σ+112dd⊤,Σ′=Σ+R24I,R=fA|1z−1zf|.\Sigma' = \Sigma + \tfrac{1}{12} d\,d^\top,\qquad \Sigma' = \Sigma + \tfrac{R^2}{4} I,\quad R = f\,A\,\left|\tfrac1z - \tfrac1{z_f}\right|.

The displacement dd has two sources: what a skeleton moved the splat over the shutter (a per-splat buffer), and a view-space step that combines camera and prim motion (viewStep). Both are projected by the same rows JWJW as the covariance. The update is checked against the same rasteriser run at N instants and averaged on the device (§2.5.3).

Antialiasing filter and the opacity it pays back. The covariance is then dilated by 0.3 px², as in Mip-Splatting’s 2D filter. Each convolution spreads the splat, so its peak must drop by k=det⁡Σstill/det⁡Σ′k = \sqrt{\det\Sigma_{\text{still}}/\det\Sigma'} to keep its energy. One square root covers the filter, the shutter and the lens together. Scaling alone loses mass, however, because a splat is drawn only out to where it falls below 1/255, and the spread enlarged that area by 1/k1/k. A converted plane meant to cover 0.25 covered 0.18 at a twentieth of the screen. The opacity is therefore paid back as

α′={kα+(1−k)/255kα≥1/255kαotherwise.\alpha' = \begin{cases} k\alpha + (1-k)/255 & k\alpha \ge 1/255\\ k\alpha & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This keeps the sharp splat’s covered area (paidOpacity). Paying back every splat would also draw those the filter had made too faint to keep. That raised a distant converted pawn from 8.7 to 29.8 ms (debug build), and the rule above costs 11.0 ms there.

Footprint and tiles. A splat is cut at the squared Mahalanobis radius

rmax⁡2(α)=min⁡(2ln⁡(255α),9),r^2_{\max}(\alpha) = \min\big(2\ln(255\,\alpha),\ 9\big),

which is where its own contribution falls below 1/255, and never beyond 3σ. The tile count is exact rather than taken from a bounding box. For each tile in the axis-aligned extent, the minimum of the quadratic form q(δ)=δ⊤Σ′−1δq(\delta)=\delta^\top \Sigma'^{-1}\delta over the box of the tile’s pixel centres is computed in closed form: since qq is convex, it is either 0 or lies on an edge, where it is a clamped 1D quadratic. The emit stage repeats the same test. Because the blend cuts at the same radius, no pixel outside a counted tile could have been touched, so tile edges are seamless. A culled slot writes the reason for culling into its unused depth-key word (edit, depth, no area, faint, off-screen, no tile), and the viewer’s Gaussians panel counts these reasons without adding a counter to the projection.

Depth key. The key is bit_cast<uint>(z) >> 7, which is order-preserving for positive floats and 24 bits wide.

2.5.2 Blending

One 16 × 16 thread group per tile loads the tile’s records in batches of 256 into group memory, and every pixel walks them from there. For pixel pp and splat ii with conic Qi=Σi′−1Q_i=\Sigma_i'^{-1}, centre μi\mu_i and colour cic_i:

αi(p)=min⁡(0.99,αi′exp⁡(−12(p−μi)⊤Qi(p−μi))),C=∑iciαi∏j<i(1−αj)+TCbehind.\alpha_i(p) = \min\!\Big(0.99,\ \alpha_i' \exp\!\big(-\tfrac12 (p-\mu_i)^\top Q_i (p-\mu_i)\big)\Big),\qquad C = \sum_i c_i\,\alpha_i \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j) + T\,C_{\text{behind}}.

Contributions below 1/255 are skipped. A pixel stops once T=∏(1−αj)<10−4T=\prod(1-\alpha_j) < 10^{-4}, and the group stops loading once all 256 of its pixels are done. CbehindC_{\text{behind}} is either the background or the opaque under layer of meshes and points, composited by view z: the walk ends at the first record behind the layer’s depth. Depth is written either as the alpha-weighted mean or as the first crossing of a coverage threshold. The same walk also accumulates a six-rank Cryptomatte, by id coverage, and for pruning the per-splat contribution TαT\alpha or the squared error the splat’s removal would leave (the GaussianPOP score). Specialised entry points compile the composite and matte branches out, because leaving them live in a frame that has neither cost the blend half again.

Surfels and harder kernels (fork athenea-pieces). A gaussian with one scale exactly 0 is a surfel. The rasteriser draws it by intersecting each pixel’s ray with the disc’s plane (Huang et al. 2024): the disc’s axes are stored in homogeneous screen coordinates and pulled back through the pixel’s two planes. The screen filter is a max, not a convolution: the larger of the disc and a round gaussian of variance ½ px². Its opacity is paid back by area,

α′=α⋅min⁡(1,λ1λ2λ1λ2+12−min⁡(λ1,12)min⁡(λ2,12)),\alpha' = \alpha\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2}+\tfrac12-\sqrt{\min(\lambda_1,\tfrac12)\min(\lambda_2,\tfrac12)}}\Big),

where λ1,2\lambda_{1,2} are the eigenvalues of the undilated screen covariance. A merged gaussian may carry an opacity D>1D>1. That value selects Spark’s harder kernel, 1−(1−g)A1-(1-g)^A with A=exp⁡((D2−1)/e)A=\exp((D^2-1)/e) and DD capped at 5. This is the composite of AA coincident gaussians, and its cut is widened by 0.7(D−1)σ0.7(D-1)\sigma. A hard surfel uses exp⁡(−(|u|p+|v|p)/2)\exp(-(|u|^p+|v|^p)/2), with pp fitted to its members’ kurtosis. Section 6 covers both, together with the merges that produce them.

2.6 The Gaussian ray tracer

render::GaussianRayTracer evaluates each particle in 3D at its peak response along the ray, with no EWA approximation and no 0.3 px dilation. Particles are blended front to back in order of their peak along the ray. In the particle’s whitened frame (origin oo, direction dd, with Σ−1/2\Sigma^{-1/2} applied), the peak and the entry into the cut ellipsoid are

t*=−o⋅dd⋅d,ρ=‖o+t*d‖2,tentry=t*−(rmax⁡2−ρ)/(d⋅d).t^\ast = -\frac{o\cdot d}{d\cdot d},\qquad \rho = \lVert o + t^\ast d\rVert^2,\qquad t_{\text{entry}} = t^\ast - \sqrt{(r^2_{\max}-\rho)/(d\cdot d)}.

The textbook discriminant b2−acb^2-ac cancels catastrophically for flat particles and lost 176 of 400 thin layers, which is why the peak form is used. Colour is the rasteriser’s: the harmonics evaluated once per frame in the direction from the eye to the centre.

Two routes, one integrator. Both routes feed the same Integrator (rt_integrate.slang). The Hardware route gives every particle a stretched-icosahedron proxy (3DGRT’s) in a device BVH queried with inline RayQuery. The ComputeBvh route is a Karras LBVH built on the GPU (Morton codes, radix sort, hierarchy, refit until a pass changes nothing) and traversed in compute. Each traversal records up to 256 unsorted entries, which are evaluated and ordered after traversal by a 128-bucket counting sort and then an insertion sort over an index permutation. When a ray meets more proxies than that, the segment closes at the nearest dropped entry and the next traversal resumes a relative 10⁻⁵ before it. Particles peaking beyond the boundary are carried into the next segment; only when more than 64 are carried are the nearest blended early, which is the route’s single approximation.

Shadow and secondary queries. Transmittance is a product, so rt_shadow.slang takes proxies in whatever order they arrive, with no k-buffer, and stops when the product falls below its cut. 2.7 Points

PointRasterizer draws point clouds through a z-buffered raster pipeline with no sort, optionally followed by surface splatting and eye-dome lighting. On devices without rasterisation (CUDA), points are drawn as discs in the splat pipeline. In that case a record’s colour.w > 0.5 marks it as an opaque disc, which ends the walk exactly as the under layer does.

2.8 Meshes: geometry, scene and visibility

On the device, in Hydra’s order. geom::MeshBuilder triangulates Hydra topology in HdMeshUtil‘s fan order, including holes and left-handed orientation. It computes smooth normals with Hd_SmoothNormals’ formula, as per-corner cross products scattered to points through a radix sort, and expands primvars of every interpolation per corner, resolving indexed primvars and decoding doubles on the device. A height field’s normals come within 1.1·10⁻⁷ rad of a brute-force sum. world::GpuScene pools positions, indices, corners, faces and primvar values, with a 64-byte record per mesh and a 176-byte InstanceRecord per drawn copy. world::Instancing composes instancer chains exactly as Storm does, instancer⋅T⋅R⋅S⋅instanceTransform\text{instancer}\cdot T\cdot R\cdot S\cdot\text{instanceTransform}, nested as parent[i]⋅level[j]\text{parent}[i]\cdot\text{level}[j], with one dispatch for all instanced sets. For Kitchen_set_instanced’s 1462 sets this took command recording from 32 ms to 0.4 ms.

Three routes, one visibility buffer. Each route writes (instance + 1, triangle) per pixel. Shading and AOVs rebuild the hit from those two numbers with Möller–Trumbore in view space (technique/surface.slang), so all routes shade alike.

  • VisibilityRaster uses reversed infinite Z (depth =near/z= \text{near}/z, D32Float), one draw per mesh with instanceCount, and a single shared root object.
  • VisibilityTrace builds one BLAS per mesh and a TLAS per frame. A kernel writes the instance descriptors in each backend’s layout (64 B generic, 80 B OptiX, 68 B Metal).
  • VisibilityBvh builds a Karras LBVH per mesh and one over instances, for devices without RT hardware.

2.9 Materials

Textures. The TextureStore uses Hio only to read a file’s bytes. The bytes are uploaded raw and a kernel decodes them into the working space. Mips are a kernel that decodes sRGB, filters in light and re-encodes. UDIM is an indirection table. All textures sit in one ParameterBlock of 1024 slots, which Metal takes as an argument buffer. Filtering is chosen per target with __target_switch: SampleGrad where compute allows it, and an explicit level from the footprint’s wider side elsewhere (CUDA). On Metal, where both are available, they pick the same levels. A decoded texture is the file to the last bit (0 of 3404 components differ). Input colour spaces resolve through colour::ColourNames, and transforms are generated by OpenColorIO (§2.12).

MaterialX into Slang. The generator derives from MaterialX 1.39.5’s SlangShaderGenerator. Every node keeps its genglsl or genslang implementation except four kinds whose rasteriser meaning cannot hold here:

  • the surface node, which has no light loop here: its BSDF graph runs once, pushing lobes;
  • BSDF and EDF nodes, which push lobes instead of answering a light;
  • image nodes, which sample the texture table;
  • heighttonormal, which needs screen derivatives.

A BSDF value is a weight per built lobe, so mix, layer, add and multiply combine weights. Uniforms are not baked into the code: each input is read from a float blob, and the module is named by a hash of its source, so materials that differ only in values share one compiled module. Generated sizes include UsdPreviewSurface at 580 lines and 23 blob words, standard_surface at 821 and 59, and OpenPBR at 945 and 55. technique::MaterialPrograms generates one module that imports every compiled material and dispatches on a material row. A kernel that evaluates materials walks its pixels in quad order (atheneaQuadPixel), because on this device the lanes run along rows and every vertical derivative had been wrong (15 939 of 15 939 threads).

The lobe library. It contains Oren–Nayar and energy-compensated EON, Burley, translucent, dielectric (reflection, transmission, both), conductor, generalised Schlick with an F82 tint, and sheen in both its Imageworks and Zeltner forms. Each lobe has eval, sample and pdf. Microfacet lobes sample the visible normal distribution and transmission follows Walter. A material’s lobes form a LobeStack, which is sampled with one-sample MIS by the lobes’ weighted directional albedos.

Checks against MaterialX itself. The same graphs are compiled a second time in a reference variant whose closures are MaterialX’s own genglsl responses, and a kernel evaluates both for 65 536 light directions. The worst component difference over eleven graphs is 3.2·10⁻⁵. On the device, the lobes pass a χ² test of sampled directions against the pdf (393.2 on 399 degrees of freedom for the diffuse lobes) and a white furnace that agrees within 3 %.

Cutouts and bump. Visibility evaluates opacityThreshold: the raster uses a generated discarding fragment shader, and the ray routes carry the ray past a cut sample up to sixteen times. Only rows flagged as cutouts pay for this. A cutout square shows what lies behind it exactly (8464 pixels, 0 mismatches) on all three routes.

2.10 Lights

Records and units. Each UsdLux light becomes one record in world space, in the units the stage authored. Exposure, the blackbody of a colour temperature (Krystek’s fit, normalised to luminance 1) and normalize are derived in the shader. Supported kinds are sphere, disk, rect, distant with an angle, dome and cylinder, plus shaping cones and IES profiles. An IES profile is read as authored, sampled bilinearly with its declared symmetry, and modulates radiance but never density. Lights under instancers are composed on the device as meshes are.

Sampling where the light stands. A sphere or sun is sampled uniformly over the cone it subtends. A disk, rect or cylinder is sampled uniformly by area and converted to solid angle by pω=d2/(cos⁡θlA)p_\omega = d^2/(\cos\theta_l\,A). A dome is sampled either cosine-weighted about the shading normal or by warping down its image’s mip chain by luminance. The warp’s density needs no tables:

p(ω)=L(u,v)L‾2π2sin⁡θ,p(\omega) = \frac{L(u,v)}{\bar L\; 2\pi^2 \sin\theta},

where L‾\bar L is the 1 × 1 mip level’s mean. The image’s variation chooses between the two strategies: on a flat sky the warp is 20 % out where the cosine is 0.08 %. lightPdf returns the density of any direction and lightHit answers the converse question, which light a ray meets and with what radiance. Together they are what MIS needs. With athenea:chooseLights, a sample takes one light chosen through a light BVH (light_bvh.slang). Each node is a box, an orientation cone (Conty and Kulla 2018) and the power beneath it, and the BVH is built by the same compute LBVH kernels as the geometry.

Checks. Every light is held to a closed form that shares no code with the renderer: a Lambert plane under a sphere, a disk, a rect (Lambert’s edge formula), a sun and a dome. In each case 0 of 8281 pixels are beyond 2 %; the worst is 1.6 % for the disk. Each light also runs a χ² test with 10⁶ samples, binned in the light’s own support, against lightPdf integrated per bin: sphere z = −0.23, disk 0.14, rect −0.27, sun −0.29, dome −1.61, every pdf integrating to 1.0000. The dome warp needed two fixes, both found by χ² in image space. The bug reached z = 61 543 and later 1 164 589, against 0.84 after the fixes. The per-sample consistency check had passed throughout, because it compares a density with itself.

2.11 The path tracer

The path tracer is the engine’s ground truth. Its paths start from the same visibility buffer the raster shading reads, so the two techniques differ by exactly one thing: the bounces. With a lens, the tracer additionally casts its own primary rays. A hit is rebuilt by material_surface.slang, its material is evaluated into the same lobe stack, and direct light is gathered by next-event estimation (NEE). The bounce samples the stack, and the path throughput is multiplied by LobeSample.weight =f|cos⁡θ|/p= f|\cos\theta|/p.

MIS. At each vertex that continues the path by sampling its material, NEE’s sample of light kk and the material sample’s hits on lights (lightHit, including escaping rays meeting domes and suns) are weighed by the power heuristic,

wlight=pl2pl2+pm2,pl=P(k∣x)pk(ω),w_{\text{light}} = \frac{p_l^2}{p_l^2 + p_m^2},\qquad p_l = P(k\mid x)\; p_k(\omega),

where P(k∣x)P(k\mid x) is the light-choice probability (by power, or through the light BVH) and pmp_m is stackPdf. Delta lights, delta lobes and the last vertex keep a weight of 1. Emissive triangles form a light table (technique::EmissiveTable): each material row’s emission is probed once, triangles are weighted by world area times that luminance, and the choice between lights and triangles is made by power, with each side’s density carrying the other’s share. On a floor of roughness 0.2 at 32 paths, MIS reduced error against a deep frame by 4.5× under a rect, 57.7× under an imageless dome and 2.4× under a sphere. An emissive quad and an equivalent UsdLux rect agree to relMSE 4.1·10⁻⁷ at 8192 paths.

Accumulation, sampler, adaptivity. A frame is a running mean of colour × opacity. An earlier implementation took the product of means, which is unbiased only while every sample is opaque. The sampler folds pixel, stream, absolute path index, bounce and dimension through a PCG hash, so 256 paths in one pass and 4 × 64 differ by relMSE 3.9·10⁻¹⁵. Between passes the camera is jittered by a Halton (2, 3) offset inside the pixel; the first pass takes no offset. This is antialiasing at no cost. Adaptive sampling stops a pixel once

(𝔼[l2]−𝔼[l]2)/N𝔼[l]<ε\frac{\sqrt{(\mathbb E[l^2]-\mathbb E[l]^2)/N}}{\mathbb E[l]} < \varepsilon

after a minimum count. The variance used is the larger of the pixel’s own and the mean of its 3 × 3 neighbours’, which reduced the stopped pixels beyond three of their own σ from 526 to 100 of 4212. Thin lens and radial distortion come from UsdGeomCamera: the lens radius is f/(2N)f/(2N), and rays are scaled by 1+k1r2+k2r41+k_1r^2+k_2r^4.

2.13 USD and Hydra

The hdAthenea plugin implements HdRenderDelegate with rprims for meshes, basis curves, points, ParticleField (Gaussian splats) and volumes; sprims for cameras, materials, the six UsdLux light types, ext computations and coordinate systems; and bprims for render buffers and render settings. 2.15 Verification without a CPU oracle

Every image comparison is a kernel. ReferenceRenderer is the ground truth for splats: every pixel considers every splat, sorts exactly what reaches it (depth, then index, the rasteriser’s tie rule) and blends front to back, with no tiles, ranges or pairs. A comparison is valid only when no pixel overflows its 1024 contributors. renderPeaks is the ray tracer’s reference, sorting exactly by peak. The metrics read back to the host are compareImages (p99, max and the number of pixels over 2, in 8-bit sRGB codes), compareHdr (relMSE =(a−b)2/(b2+10−2)¯=\overline{(a-b)^2/(b^2+10^{-2})} and the relative p99) and countDifferent for id buffers. The ray tracer is held to its reference at p99 ≤ 1 with at most 0.05 % of pixels over 2. Against the rasteriser, the tolerance holds only where the two definitions agree: p99 2 for orthographic sparse scenes, where EWA is exact, and 4 for perspective scenes with particles a few pixels across. 2.16 Limitations

  • Splats are not yet inside the path tracer’s secondary rays. A splats-only stage is traced whole by GaussianRayTracer. With meshes, splats are composited over the path-traced surfaces by depth; they cast shadows onto meshes, but a bounce ray does not see them.
  • The rasteriser’s motion and lens blur are moment-matched gaussians, not plateaus or bokeh discs: up to 38 % too peaked for long streaks.
  • Clouds whose gaussians are a few hundredths of a pixel across vanish from the raster (5·10⁻⁶ of the frame against 3·10⁻⁴ for the mesh and the tracer); levels of detail are the remedy (Section 6).
  • Raster mesh visibility pays about 8 ms of host time per 1800 draws.
  • Material compilation is synchronous.
  • The TLAS is rebuilt every frame.
  • Lens distortion beyond k1,k2k_1, k_2 is not applied.

3. From USD meshes to Gaussians (mesh2splat)

The conversion turns a USD stage of polygonal meshes and their materials into a cloud of anisotropic 3D Gaussians that the rest of the system draws, bakes, decimates and streams. It is invoked as athenea mesh2splat STAGE, and it is the core of the product: everything the phone eventually plays back was a triangle of a mesh first. This section describes how the stage is read, how large a Gaussian’s cell is, how each Gaussian is placed, shaped and coloured, how the conversion handles the places where a surface stops or folds, how it captures a layered physically based material, how it treats glass, and what was measured on the Chevrolet Corvette C7 asset that drives most of the project. The path-traced bake that follows emission (the transfer) is the subject of Section 4 and is touched on here only where it constrains the conversion.

Numbers in this section come from docs/decisions.md and docs/pieces.md of the repository, from the conversion logs under ~/luc/athenea-renders/, and from the source (plugins/mesh2splat/mesh2splat.slang, apps/athenea/src/CmdMesh2Splat.cpp, shaders/athenea/usd/mesh2splat_*.slang). All measurements were taken on an Apple M5 Pro (Metal) unless said otherwise.

3.1 Origin, and where the conversion runs

The emission algorithm is that of Electronic Arts’ mesh2splat (BSD-3): every triangle is projected onto the axis plane its normal points along most, the projection is rasterised at a fixed resolution, and each covered texel becomes one Gaussian lying in the triangle’s plane, as wide as a texel measured in the world. The original is an OpenGL geometry and fragment shader pair; here it is one compute kernel in which each thread owns a triangle and walks the cells of its projected bounding box.

The kernel is not a module of the engine but an AOFX effect, the bundle plugins/mesh2splat, loaded through the same host registry every program shares (the AOFX ABI, Section 2.14). The conversion is a stateless kernel over triangles, which is exactly the shape of an AOFX effect, and making it one keeps a converter out of the renderer’s modules and lets the same bundle run in other AOFX hosts. The cost is a boundary: an effect is handed pictures, so a mesh travels as a picture of numbers, six float4 per triangle — (𝒑,u)(\mathbf p, u) and (𝒏,v)(\mathbf n, v) for each corner, in world space (mesh_pack.slang) — and every texture a material names travels as rows of linear float4 sampled out of the engine’s texture table (texture_rows.slang). Neither is a copy: the pictures live in the gpe image storage that adopts the engine’s Metal device, and Context::renderView gives a view of the same bytes, so the packing kernel writes where the effect reads. The records the effect writes are gathered into the cloud’s device buffers (mesh2splat_gather), the cloud’s bounds are folded on the device (mesh2splat_span), and the bake reads them in place. Only counters and the final USD arrays cross to the processor.

The command is therefore a pipeline of device passes orchestrated by the host:

  1. read the stage (usd::MeshStage) and split meshes into pieces;
  2. pack each piece into a mesh picture, fold boxes, choose each piece’s cell (mesh2splat_cells);
  3. mark hard edges and closed shells per mesh (mesh2splat_edges), classify glass (mesh2splat_sheet);
  4. run the Mesh2Splat effect per piece — count, scan, emit — into records;
  5. optionally bake (radiance, or a transfer) at every Gaussian with the path tracer;
  6. write a ParticleField3DGaussianSplat stage (or a .athc) atomically.

3.2 Reading the stage

usd::MeshStage walks the stage with UsdShade directly rather than through Hydra: the conversion wants no render index, and the material side is built on the stage’s own bindings.

Selection. --prim P restricts conversion to meshes at or under P; --hide P (repeatable) authors session invisibility on P and everything below it before the read. A mesh that computes invisible at the read’s time code is skipped, so the conversion converts what the renderer draws. --hide affects the conversion only: the bake traces the whole stage, so a part converted with every other part hidden still receives the others’ shadows and inter-reflections. This is what allows the Corvette to be converted in groups (Section 3.11).

Instancing. A PointInstancer prototype’s mesh is emitted once per instance of the innermost instancer that holds it, at (mesh relative to its prototype root) × (instance transform, prototype root included) × (instancer to world); the built mesh buffers are shared between copies. Before this, the chess set’s sixteen pawns became two. Nested instancers are expanded at the innermost level only.

Time and pose. --time T sets the time code at which positions, transforms and primvars (including their indices) are read and at which the bake traces. A skinned mesh’s points do not animate in USD, so the stage is first posed with UsdSkelBakeSkinning into the session layer over [T,T][T, T], which costs one pose and leaves the file untouched; a stage without a SkelRoot is unchanged and converts byte-identically. With --skinned the Gaussians are built in the bind pose and each keeps four joints and their weight gradients; that path is described in Section 5.

Pieces. A mesh whose materialBind GeomSubsets bind different materials is converted as several pieces: each subset’s triangles, and those no subset claims. A piece’s triangles are listed on the device in the mesh’s order (flag, prefix sum, scatter) and packed as if they were a mesh, so the effect is untouched. A test mesh with two faces bound red and blue by subsets and the mesh bound green converts to 272 red and 272 blue Gaussians and none green; before the change it was all green.

Materials in their own words. The bound surface — MaterialX standard_surface, OpenPBR open_pbr_surface, gltf_pbr or UsdPreviewSurface — is read with its own input names and its own defaults (standard_surface 0.8 grey at roughness 0.2, OpenPBR roughness 0.3, glTF a fully rough metal, UsdPreviewSurface 0.18 at 0.5), following connections through node graphs and interface inputs to the image node that produces a value. A value computed by a graph (a mix, a noise) has no answer without shading a point, so the default stands and a warning is printed; a TX bake later reads such fields back from the compiled material on the device. A texture’s colour space is taken from the file input’s colorSpace metadata (or sourceColorSpace), and maps are capped at --texture-size (1024 by default): a map is a float4 picture on the device, so a 4k map would occupy 268 MB.

3.3 The size of a cell

Everything downstream scales with the cell hh, the world length one Gaussian stands for. The conversion offers four ways to choose it, in increasing order of preference.

Per model (--density per-model, the default when no camera is given). h=R/Nh = R / N, where NN is --resolution (512 by default) and RR the wider of the projection plane’s two ranges of the model’s box — the original’s orthogonalUvs. Two defects followed: a 16-unit floor diluted the car standing on it, and a panel facing forward walked a cell 2.5 times finer than the hood, because its projection plane is the car’s short side.

Per mesh (--density per-mesh). h=Lm/Nh = L_m / N with LmL_m the longest side of the mesh’s own box, the same for every triangle of it (cellByLongest), clamped to [hmin,hmax][h_{\min}, h_{\max}] given by --cell-min/--cell-max; left at 0 they derive from the model’s cell hMh_M: hmax=hMh_{\max} = h_M, hmin=hM/8h_{\min} = h_M/8, a density ratio of 64 between the finest and coarsest part. The budget. --max-splats (2 M by default) is shared over the whole stage. Every piece is first counted (a run of the effect with room for one Gaussian, whose count and scan cover everything a run would write); where the total WW exceeds the budget BB, piece ii wanting wiw_i gets si=max⁡(1,Bwi/W)s_i = \max(1, B w_i / W) and walks a cell wi/si\sqrt{w_i / s_i} times coarser. A cell kk times coarser covers the surface with k2k^2 fewer Gaussians, so proportional shares and uniform coarsening are the same thing. On two cards wanting about 1 300 under a budget of 800 the shares came out 356 and 361; spent in mesh order, the second card held about 145.

Per-triangle and per-run limits. A triangle walks at most --max-cells cells of its projected bounding box (2182^{18} by default); cells beyond are counted and reported as a warning. At 4736 px the Corvette’s Infinite_Black lost 359 628 cells this way (visible holes) and was re-baked with --max-cells 1048576. A run’s output picture is capped at 2 M Gaussians (kRunCeiling, 192 MB); a piece wanting more is converted in slices — the effect reports the first triangle the ceiling cut into, and the host reruns from there with firstTriangle — giving the same array a single run would have written.

3.4 One Gaussian per cell

The projection. For a triangle (𝒂,𝒃,𝒄)(\mathbf a, \mathbf b, \mathbf c) with geometric normal 𝒏̂g\hat{\mathbf n}_g, the two axes (i,j)(i, j) along which 𝒏̂g\hat{\mathbf n}_g points least define the projection; a point maps to 𝒐(𝒙)=(xi−bmin⁡,i,xj−bmin⁡,j)/R\mathbf o(\mathbf x) = (x_i - b_{\min,i},\, x_j - b_{\min,j}) / R, with RR the range in use (the model’s, the mesh’s longest side, or the camera-derived box). A cell is 1/N1/N of that unit square, i.e. h=R/Nh = R/N in the world, bounded as in Section 3.3. A Gaussian is emitted at every cell whose centre falls inside the projected triangle, located by its barycentric coordinates 𝝀\boldsymbol\lambda, at the world point 𝝁=λ0𝒂+λ1𝒃+λ2𝒄\boldsymbol\mu = \lambda_0 \mathbf a + \lambda_1 \mathbf b + \lambda_2 \mathbf c.

The sizes. With E=[𝒃−𝒂,𝒄−𝒂]E = [\mathbf b - \mathbf a,\; \mathbf c - \mathbf a] and O=[𝒐b−𝒐a,𝒐c−𝒐a]O = [\mathbf o_b - \mathbf o_a,\; \mathbf o_c - \mathbf o_a], the Jacobian of the projection’s inverse is J=EO−1=[𝒋u,𝒋v]J = E\,O^{-1} = [\mathbf j_u, \mathbf j_v] (guarded at |det⁡O|>10−24|\det O| > 10^{-24}). The two in-plane standard deviations and the third are

su=min⁡(‖𝒋u‖σ1N,ℓmax),sv=min⁡(‖𝒋v‖σ1N,ℓmax),sn=φmin⁡(su,sv), s_u = \min\!\left(\lVert \mathbf j_u \rVert\,\sigma\,\tfrac{1}{N},\; \ell_{\max}\right), \qquad s_v = \min\!\left(\lVert \mathbf j_v \rVert\,\sigma\,\tfrac{1}{N},\; \ell_{\max}\right), \qquad s_n = \varphi\,\min(s_u, s_v),

where σ\sigma is --sigma, ℓmax\ell_{\max} the triangle’s longest edge and φ\varphi --flatness. Three details were read from the original source rather than guessed: the Jacobian is over the triplanar coordinates, not over texture coordinates; the 1/N1/N factor is applied by the original’s exporter, not by a shader (without it every Gaussian is the size of the model); and the frame is not the Jacobian’s columns but the triangle’s longest edge 𝒕̂\hat{\mathbf t}, the interpolated shading normal 𝒏̂=normalize⁡(∑kλk𝒏k)\hat{\mathbf n} = \operatorname{normalize}(\sum_k \lambda_k \mathbf n_k), and their cross product. The covariance is therefore

Σ=Rframediag⁡(su2,sv2,sn2)Rframe⊤,Rframe=[𝒕̂⟂,𝒏̂×𝒕̂⟂,𝒏̂], \Sigma = R_{\text{frame}}\,\operatorname{diag}(s_u^2, s_v^2, s_n^2)\,R_{\text{frame}}^{\!\top}, \qquad R_{\text{frame}} = [\hat{\mathbf t}_\perp,\; \hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf t}_\perp,\; \hat{\mathbf n}],

with 𝒕̂⟂\hat{\mathbf t}_\perp the edge made orthogonal to 𝒏̂\hat{\mathbf n}. The bound ℓmax\ell_{\max} was added after 1 859 slivers of a BMW, seen almost edge-on by their own projection, produced Jacobian columns twenty orders of magnitude too long and drew white spikes metres long.

The original wrote 10−710^{-7} model units as the third size, which made a Gaussian’s thinness depend on the model’s scale: a 66 mm pawn traced correctly while a 100-unit fox became a ghost under the ray tracer. The third size is now the fraction φ\varphi (default 0.1) of the smaller in-plane size. --flatness 0 writes surfels — Gaussians with one scale exactly zero, the 2D Gaussian-splatting disc — which the rasteriser draws by a ray–plane intersection per pixel and the path tracer clamps to 10−410^{-4} of the widest axis.

Opacity as coverage. A point of a converted surface lies under several Gaussians at once, so an opacity written straight into each over-covers: a mask of 0.5 covered 96 % of what stood behind it. A triangle that catches no cell. A triangle smaller than a cell need contain no cell centre; rasterised, it vanishes. Converted, that removed the Buick’s grille and window rubbers (80 137 triangles became 34 011 Gaussians). Such a triangle now gets one Gaussian at its centroid, sized by its own second moments (m2sOwnSize): the uniform triangle’s covariance Σ△=112∑k𝒅k𝒅k⊤\Sigma_\triangle = \tfrac{1}{12}\sum_k \mathbf d_k \mathbf d_k^\top (corners about the centroid, in the tangent plane), widened as a cell is, Σ=12σ2Σ△\Sigma = 12\sigma^2 \Sigma_\triangle, with the wide axis clamped to [0.05smax,smax][0.05\,s_{\max}, s_{\max}] of the cell’s size and the narrow axis to at least 0.02 of the wide. Before this, a panel’s rolled lip — a strip of sub-cell triangles — drew a band of cell-wide beads three pixels thick along every Corvette edge where the path tracer draws a hairline. In the Corvette bakes, glass and tyres come out at about one Gaussian per triangle at both 640 and 1280 px.

Deterministic order. The original appended with an atomic counter, so two conversions of the pawn produced the same 729 073 Gaussians in different orders. The emit is now three passes — count each triangle’s covered cells, an exclusive scan in one 256-thread workgroup, write each triangle’s Gaussians at its offset — sharing one function (m2sTriangleOf) so that counting and writing cannot disagree. Two conversions are now byte-identical, a budget keeps the first Gaussians in mesh order, and Gaussian ii is the same Gaussian from one pose to the next. A record that decodes to nothing is written as an empty slot rather than dropped, so array indices stay aligned.

3.5 Where a surface stops or folds

A Gaussian is a cell wide, and on the Corvette a cell is a pixel of the conversion camera. A part narrower than that — the flange a hood folds down into its gap — still received cell-wide Gaussians in its own plane; they reached out of the gap over the paint beside it, carrying the gap’s baked darkness, and every panel edge became a thick black wavy line where the path tracer draws a thin one.

Hard edges (mesh2splat_edges.slang). Per mesh, over all its pieces, every edge and corner is keyed by where its points stand and radix-sorted on the device; each run of equal edge keys is one edge. An edge is hard when it is open (one triangle uses it: the part stops or another mesh begins), when the two triangles sharing it fold by more than 40∘40^\circ (kCreaseDegrees), or when more than two triangles share it (non-manifold). Because keys are positions over the whole mesh, a boundary between two materials on one smooth surface is not an edge. Each hard edge becomes a line with an inside, (𝒎̂,c)(\hat{\mathbf m}, c) with inside 𝒎̂⋅𝒙≥c\hat{\mathbf m}\cdot\mathbf x \ge c — the plane through the edge, orthogonal to its triangle, facing the third corner — known to its own triangle as a bit and told to every triangle touching either end (up to five lines each), since a Gaussian near a corner can stand beside the next triangle’s hard edge.

Holding the reach (m2sHoldEdges, --edge-reach ρ\rho, default 1.5). For each line in the Gaussian’s plane, let 𝒎\mathbf m be its normal in the tangent basis, CC the in-plane covariance, r2=𝒎⊤C𝒎r^2 = \mathbf m^\top C\,\mathbf m the variance across the line and dd the centre’s signed distance to it. The size allowed across the line is a=max⁡(d/ρ,0.05r)a = \max(d/\rho,\; 0.05\,r), and where r>ar > a

C←C−κ(C𝒎)(C𝒎)⊤,κ=1−a2/r2r2, C \leftarrow C - \kappa\,(C\mathbf m)(C\mathbf m)^\top, \qquad \kappa = \frac{1 - a^2/r^2}{r^2},

which makes 𝒎⊤C𝒎=a2\mathbf m^\top C\,\mathbf m = a^2 exactly while keeping the variance along the line’s conjugate direction, so a strip of Gaussians still covers the strip. Lines not lying in the Gaussian’s plane (the flange seen from the paint it folds off), and lines the centre stands more than a quarter size behind, are ignored; lines are applied in a canonical order so the result does not depend on discovery order. The new covariance is re-diagonalised into a tangent and two sizes. The count of Gaussians is unchanged — only their shapes are.

Closed shells. The same pass counts edges not shared by exactly two triangles, one counter read back per mesh. A mesh is marked closed when

nopen<21.5T, n_{\text{open}} < 2\sqrt{1.5\,T},

TT its triangles (1.5T1.5T approximates its welded edges), and its Gaussians carry primvars:athenea:splat:closed = 1, which allows a player or a cut to drop nodes facing away. A fixed share of edges was tried first (2 %) and failed: an open sheet’s border grows as the square root of its area, so a fine enough tube passed as closed — the rims’ barrels (1 %) were culled and light showed between the spokes on the phone. Against the square root, the doors (no open edge), logos (1.4) and calipers (1.5) stay closed and rims (4.0), tyres (3.1), hood (3.1) and bumper (4.4) do not: 102 of the Corvette’s 192 meshes. A car’s door is two skins; drawn without culling, the inner skin sorted in front of the outer in patches, a net of dark cracks at phone size.

Measured. The Corvette’s TX base at 640 px of /World/Camera, rebaked with edges held (default 1.5) and as a control with --edge-reach 0, both 5 380 804 Gaussians; day frame at 1920 × 1080 against the mesh path trace:

3.6 Material capture

Each Gaussian record carries position, coverage-derived opacity, three sizes, a rotation and a colour, followed — depending on what the cloud needs — by the shading normal and metallic, roughness, transmission and texture coordinates; four joints and their gradients; three relief entries for displacement; an emission entry; four layer entries; and the shape operator.

Base. The colour is the material’s base colour times its base weight, times the albedo map where there is one (decoded to linear). Metallic and roughness are the material’s constants, multiplied by the packed map’s channels where there is one (blue metallic, green roughness), as glTF multiplies its factors; a missing channel is a factor of one. The original defaulted to (0.1,0.5)(0.1, 0.5) from the map alone, so every material without a map became the same plastic; with the material’s own values passed, a converted quad under a head-on light reads 0.5009 against the mesh’s 0.5032.

Shading normal. A normal map is read in the frame its UVs give — ∂𝒑/∂u\partial\mathbf p/\partial u from the triangle’s positions and UVs, orthonormalised against the normal, as the mesh shader does — and the result is kept as the Gaussian’s shading normal (primvars:athenea:splat:normal) beside its geometric frame. Previously the map was read against the longest edge, and every facet of the pawn’s marble turned its veins by its own angle. The disc itself is not turned unless --normal-map-turns is given, which would open the surface where the relief is steep.

Layers (proposal 026). A Gaussian carries the specular weight, colour and index; the coat’s weight, roughness and index; the sheen’s colour times its weight and its roughness; and the coat darkening switch — OpenPBR’s units, three words per splat on the device. Up to three maps on these inputs are sampled per Gaussian (m2sLayersAt). This was needed because the Corvette’s paint (Car_Paint_Main) is OpenPBR metalness 1 over a base of about 0.05 under a clear coat of weight 1: everything the eye sees of it is the lacquer, and without the coat a converted car came out nearly black.

Schlick metals. OpenPBR and glTF metals are MaterialX generalized_schlick_bsdf (colour head-on, specular colour at 82∘82^\circ), not a conductor of the artistic index; for a dark metal the two differ by a factor of two at grazing angles (at 60∘60^\circ: 0.172 for the conductor, 0.077 for the Schlick from F0=0.047F_0 = 0.047). The material’s kind is written per Gaussian (primvars:athenea:splat:schlickMetal) and the renderer reflects it with the DFG fit of its two ends. An earlier variant of the same confusion made the bake drop every OpenPBR metal as dielectric polish: a Mustang’s front bumper stored the constant that decodes to black in all 27 016 Gaussians; with Schlick lobes of F0>0.2F_0 > 0.2 kept as body, the bumper’s mean colour became (0.45, 0.51, 0.61).

Emission and transmission. Emission is the material’s colour times its map, linear and unbounded, in an entry of its own. Transmission TT and transmission colour 𝒄T\mathbf c_T are a channel the original did not have (glass came out opaque white). The colour is taken towards the transmission colour,

𝒄←lerp⁡(𝒄,lerp⁡(𝟏,𝒄,wT)𝒄T,T), \mathbf c \leftarrow \operatorname{lerp}\!\bigl(\mathbf c,\; \operatorname{lerp}(\mathbf 1, \mathbf c, w_T)\,\mathbf c_T,\; T\bigr),

with wT=1w_T = 1 for glTF (transmission tinted by the base colour) and 0 for OpenPBR and standard_surface. The opacity is the coverage mapping of Section 3.4 applied to o⋅omat⋅mask⋅lerp⁡(1,g,T)o\cdot o_{\text{mat}}\cdot\text{mask}\cdot \operatorname{lerp}(1, g, T), gg being what a fully transmitting surface still covers (Section 3.8).

The material roughness mark. The stored roughness may be widened by the specular filter (Section 3.7). The material’s own roughness before widening is written separately, primvars:athenea:splat:materialRoughness (−1 under a roughness map, read as sharp). The player uses it to decide whether the polish lobe answers its occluded part from the baked reflected field (material roughness at least 0.3: paint, 0.34) or keeps it dark (chrome 0, rough metal 0.22); in the decimated tree it is packed into the pbr word (bit 26 present, bits 27–31 the value in five bits).

3.7 The specular filter (Toksvig widening)

A Gaussian has one normal. Where a strip of chrome curves under it, the path-traced pixel averages a highlight that moves across the footprint and shows a thin line; the Gaussian shows the light its single normal meets, at full brightness, over its whole footprint. On the Corvette the grille trim — four pixels high — came back as a row of white beads, and the chrome read 1.6 times the path trace while a chrome ball validated within 1 %.

The conversion therefore widens the roughness by the spread of normals under the Gaussian (m2sSpread, m2sWidened). The turn rate is the largest angle between corner normals per unit corner distance, over the triangle’s three edges, and the spread is that rate times the Gaussian’s wider size:

s=kmaxe∠(𝒏e0,𝒏e1)‖𝒑e1−𝒑e0‖max⁡(su,sv), s = k\,\max_{e}\frac{\angle(\mathbf n_{e_0}, \mathbf n_{e_1})}{\lVert \mathbf p_{e_1} - \mathbf p_{e_0} \rVert}\,\max(s_u, s_v),

with kk = --specular-filter (1 with --transfer, 0 otherwise). With perceptual roughness rr and GGX α=r2\alpha = r^2, the spread is added to the slope variance (Toksvig):

α′2=α2+2s2,r′=saturate⁡((r4+2s2)1/4). \alpha'^2 = \alpha^2 + 2 s^2, \qquad r' = \operatorname{saturate}\!\left((r^4 + 2 s^2)^{1/4}\right).

A flat triangle, or one whose corners share a normal, is unchanged. The coat’s roughness is widened the same way unless the cloud carries the shape operator.

The shape operator. For a transfer the conversion also writes, in the Gaussian’s own axes (𝒖,𝒗)(\mathbf u, \mathbf v), the symmetric shape operator SS with d𝒏=Sd𝒙\mathrm d\mathbf n = S\,\mathrm d\mathbf x, solved from the corners’ normal differences against their position differences (m2sShape, stored as (Suu,Suv,Svv)(S_{uu}, S_{uv}, S_{vv})). The renderer uses it to turn the reflection across the footprint pixel by pixel (the per-pixel “sharp lobe”). Widening the coat as well would blur twice, so where SS is written the coat is not widened, and the player reads the sharp lobes at the unwidened roughness r0=max⁡(r′4−2s2,0)1/4r_0 = \max(r'^4 - 2s^2, 0)^{1/4} (unwidened), with ss carried per Gaussian.

Measured. At 640 px the coat came out at roughness 0.17–0.24 with 5–14 % high-pass on folds; at 1280 px the spread under each Gaussian is smaller and the coat’s roughness comes out at exactly 0, so the widening matters less as the bake gets finer. Turning it off is nevertheless worse (Section 3.11): with --specular-filter 0 a dashed dark line appears on the sill’s fold and the chrome strip sparkles.

3.8 Glass

A Gaussian cannot refract, but it can let what is behind it through by its opacity, reflect through its specular lobe, and carry an index of refraction that the renderer bends by (primvars:athenea:splat:ior, one per cloud; with layers each Gaussian bends by its own specular index). The conversion’s job is to decide, per transmitting piece, whether the glass is a solid (a body: a ball, a lens) or a thin wall (a sheet or slab whose two faces bend nothing), because the two are drawn differently.

Classification (classifySheets, mesh2splat_sheet.slang), for every transmitting piece whose material does not already declare itself thin-walled. On the device, the piece’s edges are keyed by position, sorted, and their runs counted; the signed volume VV (tetrahedra to the origin) and the area AA are summed. Then

  • open: more than 1/2001/200 of the edges have one triangle, or any edge has more than two;
  • slab: closed, and the thickness 2V/A2V/A is below four of the model’s cells or below 0.02A0.02\sqrt A (2V/A2V/A is a slab’s gap and two thirds of a ball’s radius).

An open or slab piece is read thin-walled — unless it is tinted (transmission colour luminance below 0.9), which stays solid for now, since a thin wall lets the background through grey. The Corvette’s glass turned out to be closed slabs 3–4 mm thick (research proposal 055), which the edge rule alone called solid; the thickness rule reads them. Each decision is printed, e.g. for the rear light lens: “tinted, kept solid glass: 200 of its 17284 edges open, 0 shared by more than two; 1.199 thick (2V/A) against a cell of 0.007921”. --thin-glass MAT and --solid-glass MAT override per material.

Coverage. A solid glass covers --glass-opacity of what is behind it (default 0.6; a TX lens answers its background from the domes alone, so a glass that covered fully hid every other light shining through it). A thin wall covers exactly what the sheet reflects head-on, both faces with inter-reflection:

gthin=min⁡(2R1+R,0.998),R=(n−1n+1)2, g_{\text{thin}} = \min\!\left(\frac{2R}{1+R},\; 0.998\right), \qquad R = \left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)^2,

and the frame scales its reflection back by 1/R1/R. At the windscreen’s index of 1.16 that is an opacity of 0.0055, and the rasteriser draws such sheets at an opacity of at least 0.1 with colour scaled down by the same factor, which keeps the product α𝒄\alpha\,\mathbf c — the reflection — intact (windshield relMSE 2.26 → 0.89 under the workshop sky).

3.10 Validation against the path trace

3.12 Limitations

  • A Gaussian has one normal and one cell’s footprint: a chrome tube a few pixels across is still 38 % brighter than the path trace on the front bumper, and the bumper’s lower lip is ragged.
  • Lamps carry no emission in the transfer bake; headlights are dark against the path trace.
  • A tinted thin glass is converted solid, and a cloud keeps a single index unless it carries layers.
  • The coverage mapping assumes the cell lattice’s overlap 2πσ22\pi\sigma^2; sub-cell triangles and displaced, split cells overlap otherwise and are not corrected.
  • Nested point instancers are expanded only at the innermost level; motion authored as velocities is read at the sample.
  • Device memory bounds a part at about 5 M leaves for the merge tree that follows; larger parts are converted at a coarser camera resolution.

4. Light transport baked into Gaussians (transfer) and relighting

A cloud converted from a mesh can carry its light in two ways. A radiance bake stores, per Gaussian, the light that arrived under the stage’s own illumination; it is exact for that illumination and wrong under any other. A transfer stores instead how each Gaussian’s surroundings would carry an arbitrary environment to it, and a frame combines that geometry with whatever sky and lights it has. This section describes the transfer as athenea bakes and evaluates it in its TX form (“task TX”: a transfer right under any sky and any light), the reflection model built on top of it, the per-pixel treatment of sharp lobes, and what each piece was measured to buy against a path trace of the source mesh. Everything described runs on the GPU: the bake is the scene’s own path tracer in a transfer mode, and the evaluation is the splat projection and blend kernels (shaders/athenea/splat/splat_relight.slang, splat_project.slang, splat_blend.slang).

4.1 Notation

A Gaussian has a position 𝒑\mathbf{p}, a unit normal 𝒏\mathbf{n} (its shortest axis, or a stored shading normal, turned towards the eye), an albedo ρ\rho, a metalness mm, a perceptual roughness rr with GGX parameter α=r2\alpha = r^2, and optionally a clear coat (weight wcw_c, roughness rcr_c, index ηc\eta_c), a sheen and a transmission. 𝝎o\boldsymbol{\omega}_o is the direction to the eye and 𝒎=reflect⁡(−𝝎o,𝒏)\mathbf{m} = \operatorname{reflect}(-\boldsymbol{\omega}_o, \mathbf{n}) the mirror direction. YkY_k are the real spherical harmonics, indexed k=l2+l+mk = l^2 + l + m; degree 2 has 9 coefficients and degree 3 has 16. A sky has radiance coefficients LkL_k (rgb), a sun of direction 𝒔\mathbf{s} and irradiance E⊙E_\odot extracted from it, and a prefiltered map L̃(𝝎,r)\tilde L(\boldsymbol{\omega}, r).

4.3 The environment at draw time

technique::Environment prepares each dome in three forms, all buffers read through one module (light/environment.slang) by both the rasteriser and the tracer.

Spherical harmonics of radiance. env_project sums the texels of one level of the dome’s mip chain, each weighed by the lat-long solid angle 2π2sin⁡θΔuΔv2\pi^2 \sin\theta\,\Delta u\,\Delta v (the measure the dome’s importance sampling uses):

Lk=∑texelsL(𝝎t)Yk(𝝎t)2π2sin⁡θtΔuΔv.L_k = \sum_{\text{texels}} L(\boldsymbol{\omega}_t)\, Y_k(\boldsymbol{\omega}_t)\, 2\pi^2 \sin\theta_t\, \Delta u\, \Delta v .

Sixteen coefficients are projected (degree 3); the irradiance of an untransferred body reads the first nine, convolved at read time with the clamped-cosine zonal weights {π,2π/3,π/4}\{\pi, 2\pi/3, \pi/4\}. A constant sky projects back to πL\pi L of irradiance at every normal: 6.2841 against 6.2832 (0.089 %) over 64 directions.

The sun, taken out. A disc a thousand times brighter than the sky is not a degree-2 function; projected, it smears into a lobe sixty degrees wide. env_sun finds the brightest region whose light is as concentrated as a disc of its solid angle (light-weighted mean square angle about its centre at most twice a disc’s, θcone2/2\theta_{\text{cone}}^2/2), takes its texels out of both the harmonics and the prefilter’s source, and returns a direction 𝒔\mathbf{s}, an irradiance E⊙E_\odot and a half angle. The irradiance is summed over exactly the texels the harmonics skip, at the same level and with the same measure, so what leaves the coefficients arrives as a light (closed form 0.1119 against 0.1113).

The prefiltered map. The sky without its sun, convolved with a GGX lobe into an octahedral mip chain of eight levels, level kk standing for roughness (k/7)2(k/7)^2 (0, 0.020, 0.082, 0.184, 0.327, 0.510, 0.735, 1). The base side matches the dome’s own texel, S=2WH/πS = \sqrt{2WH/\pi}, rounded down to a power of two in [256,2048][256, 2048]. The prefilter uses filtered importance sampling (Krivanek and Colbert): each GGX sample reads the source one level coarser than its own solid angle asks, never narrower than the texel it writes, with the sequence turned by a per-texel hash, 64 samples at level 1 doubling to 1024. The source’s lat-long mips weigh each row by cos⁡θ0−cos⁡θ1\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta_1, so every level keeps the sky’s solid-angle mean. Without these, a 1.5-degree sun in a rough level was a scatter of texel-sized blobs (a paint ball’s 4-16 px band at 2.13 times the path trace).

4.4 The bake

4.4.1 The transfer integral and its estimator

The transfer is the projection of visibility times the clamped cosine, the albedo taken as one:

Tk=1π∫S2V(𝝎)max⁡(𝒏⋅𝝎,0)Yk(𝝎)d𝝎,T_k = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{S^2} V(\boldsymbol{\omega})\, \max(\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}, 0)\, Y_k(\boldsymbol{\omega})\, d\boldsymbol{\omega},

and a frame recombines it with the sky as ⟨T,L⟩=∑kTkLk\langle T, L \rangle = \sum_k T_k L_k. It is gathered by a third variant of the path-traced kernel, kTransfer, beside the radiance bake and the camera path. At a Gaussian’s first vertex the material is replaced by the sampled direction itself: directions are stratified (a jittered N×N\sqrt{N}\times\sqrt{N} grid) and uniform over the hemisphere, density 1/2π1/2\pi, against an estimator that wants cos⁡θ/π\cos\theta/\pi, so an escaping sample is worth 2cos⁡θYk(𝝎)2\cos\theta\, Y_k(\boldsymbol{\omega}) and a blocked one nothing. Uniform rather than cosine sampling, and over the hemisphere measured as a sphere with the far half empty, because the harmonics are orthonormal only over the sphere; fitted coefficient by coefficient over a hemisphere they overshoot (the pawn came back ten times too bright). The accumulation is the projection; no fit is solved.

The same paths give two halves at no extra ray: a path that escapes right after the first vertex contributes to the direct half (scalar); one that escapes after bouncing carries the colour of what it met and contributes to the indirect half TkindT^{\text{ind}}_k (rgb). Checked against closed forms: an unoccluded point transfers the clamped cosine, (0.2820,0.3258,0.1581)(0.2820, 0.3258, 0.1581) against the exact (0.2821,0.3257,0.1577)(0.2821, 0.3257, 0.1577) for bands 0-2, and a constant sky of 0.7 reads 0.6998. 4.4.2 Open directions and the direct half by quadrature

At the first sample of each point TX traces one any-hit ray per cell of a Sc×ScS_c \times S_c octahedral grid (Sc=16S_c = 16 or 32) over the whole sphere: the far half too, starting from the far side of the surface, so a direction behind a solid meets the solid and one behind a sheet leaves. A cell’s bit is set where at least half the light gets through: the occlusion ray passes up to eight surfaces whose material transmits, weighed by transmission times colour luminance less the Fresnel at that angle (a ninth closes the way). The cells are traced from four origins over the Gaussian’s footprint (cell cc from origin (c+⌊c/Sc⌋)mod⁡4(c + \lfloor c/S_c \rfloor) \bmod 4: the centre and three points brought onto the surface within the Gaussian’s disc), so a groove narrower than a Gaussian no longer reads as a dashed line of whole shadows.

The same rays then give the direct half by quadrature (no grain), each cell weighted by its octahedral solid angle:

Tkdir=∑cτcmax⁡(𝒏⋅𝝎c,0)πΔΩcYk(𝝎c),ΔΩc=4Sc2‖𝝎c‖13,T_k^{\text{dir}} = \sum_{c} \tau_c\, \frac{\max(\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_c,0)}{\pi}\, \Delta\Omega_c\, Y_k(\boldsymbol{\omega}_c), \qquad \Delta\Omega_c = \frac{4}{S_c^2}\,\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}_c\rVert_1^3,

with τc\tau_c the fraction transmitted along the cell’s ray. Words are written four at a time as each 128 cells are traced, so the kernel holds no array the size of the grid.

4.4.3 The reflected field

A reflection needs the bounced light by direction, not integrated. For every path that leaves the Gaussian, meets the scene and later escapes, the throughput past the first vertex is the radiance that arrives along its first direction 𝝎1\boldsymbol{\omega}_1 under a white sky of radiance one. It is projected onto degree 3 of 𝝎1\boldsymbol{\omega}_1, uniformly over the domain it was drawn from:

Fj≈1N∑i:escaped after a bounceΩmeasβiwiYj(𝝎1,i),F_j \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i:\ \text{escaped after a bounce}} \Omega_{\text{meas}}\, \frac{\beta_i}{w_i}\, Y_j(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1,i}),

with βi\beta_i the throughput, wiw_i the first-vertex weight and Ωmeas=2π\Omega_{\text{meas}} = 2\pi (or 4π4\pi over the sphere). That is 16 rgb coefficients, primvars:athenea:splat:transferReflected. It costs no ray. Because FF is zero along open directions, sixteen harmonics read a closed direction beside an open one at a fraction of what stands there (the paint’s closed part was 0.0014 of 0.115). So the bake also projects the indicator of closed directions CjC_j over the same samples, and the conversion divides the two, a normalised convolution giving the mean radiance of the closed directions about each direction, falling back to the closed directions’ mean where their share is under 0.1 (blended up to 0.4), and re-projects the result over a 16 x 16 grid by cell solid angle (m2sFieldOverClosed).

For a transmitting Gaussian the first direction is drawn over the whole sphere (bakeSphereDirection: the stratum’s height stretched to [−1,1][-1,1] and the lower half mirrored through the tangent plane). Front samples keep the transfer’s estimator (4cos⁡θ4\cos\theta at density 1/4π1/4\pi); back samples feed only the field, after being bent into a solid dielectric by its index (refractInto), so the field along −𝝎o-\boldsymbol{\omega}_o holds what the eye’s ray finds through both faces. Unbent, a glass ball validated at half the path trace (0.265 against 0.541 under a white dome).

4.4.4 Slices, filtering, and the split bake

The whole TX Corvette asked for its bake’s answer at once: 35 float4 per Gaussian, 7.8 GB, with 5 GB free. Converter::transfer therefore bakes in slices whose answer is at most 1.5 GB (never under the bake’s own batch, 2192^{19}), and at most 2202^{20} Gaussians where the filter runs. Each slice’s rays are copied on the device, baked (StageRenderer::bakePointsOnDevice in transfer mode), its bounced halves filtered, written by m2sTransferInto and read back into the file’s arrays before the next. The radiance bake uses the related StageRenderer::bakeSplitOnDevice, which keeps the direct and indirect halves as separate sums so that the indirect one can be filtered before combineBake fits the two. The transfer follows the same principle: its indirect half and its field (the noisiest values, at 64 paths per Gaussian in the Corvette’s bakes) go through the splat bake filter, an a-trous filter at --bake-filter iterations (3), whose weights are position on the tangent plane, facing and prim id; the direct half, which holds the shadows, is never filtered.

4.4.5 Zonal lobes for skinned clouds

Nine harmonics are in the world; a skeleton turns the Gaussian under them. Rotating them costs a 9 x 9 per Gaussian. A zonal lobe rotates by rotating its axis, so a skinned cloud keeps two lobes, each the octahedral (u,v)(u, v) of its axis in the Gaussian’s own frame and zonal coefficients z0,z1,z2z_0, z_1, z_2: ten values (primvars:athenea:splat:transferZonal). As harmonics a lobe about 𝒂\mathbf{a} is glm=zlklYlm(𝒂)g_{lm} = z_l\, k_l\, Y_{lm}(\mathbf{a}) with kl=4π/(2l+1)k_l = \sqrt{4\pi/(2l+1)}. For a fixed axis the best coefficients are the projection zl=kl∑mflmYlm(𝒂)z_l = k_l \sum_m f_{lm} Y_{lm}(\mathbf{a}), leaving error ‖f‖2−∑lzl2\lVert f\rVert^2 - \sum_l z_l^2, so the fit (an AOFX effect, splattransferzonal) searches the axis on the device (band-1 direction, the normal, 64 Fibonacci directions, then a halving walk), fits the second lobe to the residual and refits the pair twice (Sloan 2008). At draw time splatTransferFrame turns each axis by the skinned frame and rebuilds nine world harmonics, which everything downstream reads unchanged. The bake poses the cloud at --time with the engine’s own skinner, so the frame the lobes are fitted against is the packed quaternion a renderer reads. The shadow bits stay two words, re-binned into the Gaussian’s frame. The indirect half is not kept zonal.

4.6.1 Which part of a lobe sees the sky

The field was baked under a white sky. Under a real one its pattern stands and its brightness is scaled per channel by how much more the surroundings bounce under that sky than under white (splatFieldCoupling):

4.6.4 Layers, Fresnel, metals, sheen

Environment albedo. PP is MaterialX’s ggxDirectionalAlbedo with multiple-scattering compensation, not Lazarov’s fit, which differed by up to 76 % at grazing; a dielectric uses the exact Fresnel against F0F_0, a conductor its colour at both ends, mixed by mm. Where the material’s metal is a generalized Schlick (OpenPBR, glTF), it runs from its colour head on to the specular colour at grazing (ggxEnvSchlickMetal); for a dark metal a conductor of artistic index and a Schlick differ by twice at sixty degrees, and the car’s paint read matte and bright as a conductor.

The coat’s Fresnel. Schlick is low through the middle of a dielectric’s curve (0.070 at sixty degrees where a lacquer at 1.45 reflects 0.081); a coat over black read 0.084 against the path trace’s 0.099. The coat’s albedo is the fit scaled by exact over Schlick at the eye, fading with roughness:

4.7 Glass, transmission and thin walls

A Gaussian cannot refract. What it sends on is the light from behind it: (1−t)(1-t) of the body faces the eye’s side, tt faces away, and what stands behind is seen through the Gaussian’s opacity.

  • What passes is what is not reflected: the transmitted half is weighted by 1−wspecEfit(r,η)1 - w_{\text{spec}} E_{\text{fit}}(r,\eta), with wspecw_{\text{spec}} the specular weight; one Fresnel per face, as the path tracer’s transmission lobe pays it; a ball came back half again too bright with a rim 2.5 times too hot before.
  • Rasterised TX glass sees through along −𝝎o-\boldsymbol{\omega}_o: the sky where the cells behind are open (splatConeOpen about −𝒏-\mathbf{n}) and κF\kappa F where they are closed, so a windscreen shows the cabin, blurred to degree 3. 4.11 Limitations

  • Rank-one field. The coupling puts a lit ground’s colour, not its shape, in a reflection under a sky lit from one side; at degree 3 the field resolves a horizon to about twenty degrees and blurs the car’s shadow on the ground into the far ground (in simulation: correlation 0.56 at degree 3, 0.63 at degree 5, 0.85 with a 32 x 32 octahedral map).
  • Cell resolution. 16 x 16 cells of about 11 degrees let sky through where a sharp coat mirrors the car itself (the door’s open class at 1.13); 32 x 32 cells (+96 B per Gaussian) would halve it.
  • One sun per dome; lamps inside a scene are shadowed by bits that say which ways leave the scene, not which reach the lamp. The bake has no lamp emission: the headlights are dark against the path trace.
  • Remaining defects on the 1280 px car: the chrome strip on the front bumper 38 % brighter than the path trace with a lower peak; the rim’s near-ground reflection at 0.72; the hood highlights 2.0 px off, which a curvature fitted over the footprint from the mesh would roughly halve.
  • Zonal transfers keep the direct half only, and know only the pose that was traced; occlusion between limbs in other poses is not multiplied in.

5. Animated assets: skeletons, skinning and per-part visibility (the pawn and the sparrow)

This section describes how athenea turns a rigged USD asset (UsdSkel) into a cloud of 3D Gaussians that a skeleton still drives. It covers how the cloud is converted, how it is posed on the GPU every frame, how its light and self-shadowing are kept valid under deformation, how levels of detail treat it, and how it is played back in a browser and on a phone. Two assets run through the section. The Eurasian tree sparrow (SparrowBird.usda, 609 joints, 71 animation clips) is the animated test case. The chess pawn of The Open Chess Set is the rigid control. Every number below is taken from docs/decisions.md, the source files named alongside it, or the measurement files of the web player (sparkwebGPU). Numbers that were not measured are marked as not measured.

5.1 Design principle: carry the rig, not the frames

There are two ways to animate a Gaussian cloud. The first stores every gaussian’s position, orientation and scale at every frame. The second stores the gaussians once, in the bind pose, together with the joints that carry each one, plus one transform per joint per frame. athenea uses the second, for three reasons, each measured:

  1. Size. For a plausible character of 150 k gaussians over 120 frames, the per-frame arrays cost 960 MB at half precision (1.2 GB at float). The rig (four joints per gaussian, 60 joints per frame) costs 2.9 MB. A synthetic tube (200 rings × 64 segments, 40 joints, 184 320 gaussians) was measured as written. Its file is 9.27 MB at 25 instants and 9.50 MB at 121, against about 713 MB for the same range as per-frame arrays. Five times the frames cost 230 kB.
  2. Exactness between samples. A rig is exact at every instant of the range. A sampled cloud is only as good as the reader’s interpolation.
  3. Stable identity. The posed cloud is written into buffers that outlive it, so a renderer sees the same cloud from one frame to the next. That is what lets the ray tracer refit its acceleration structure instead of rebuilding it (§5.6).

The cost of this choice is that whatever was baked in world space (radiance, spherical-harmonic transfer, visibility) stops being valid as soon as a limb moves. §5.7 and §5.8 deal with this.

5.2 Reading a rigged stage

The converter (athenea mesh2splat) does not go through Hydra. It reads meshes through its own MeshStage, which leaves two ways to handle a skinned mesh:

  • Posed. Without --skinned, the stage is posed before it is read. UsdSkelBakeSkinning writes the posed points as time samples into the session layer, over GfInterval(t, t). This costs one pose, leaves the file on disk unchanged, and leaves a stage with no SkelRoot untouched. Test: a square carried by one joint that slides (1.2, 0.4, 0) between t = 0 and t = 1 converts to bounds, folded on the device, that differ by exactly that slide.
  • Carried. With --skinned, the bind pose is read and UsdSkelBakeSkinning is skipped. Posing first would skin the geometry twice. For each mesh, MeshStage resolves its skel binding with a UsdSkelCache over every SkelRoot and one UsdSkelSkinningQuery per skinnable prim. It returns the joint influences, geomBindTransform, the skeleton path and the skeleton’s joint order.

Joint order. A mesh may list its own subset of joints in skel:joints. UsdSkel’s mapper only maps skeleton order to mesh order, so it is run over the identity to learn the skeleton index of each mesh index. Influences are always written in skeleton order, so one cloud has one joint order no matter how many meshes it was converted from. A mesh in a skinned stage that no joint carries (the sparrow stage holds a cylinder and a plane beside the bird) still gets one influence record per gaussian: four joints of zero weight, which the skinner reads as “stay in the bind pose”. This keeps rig and cloud aligned one to one.

--skinned forces --no-bake. A radiance bake holds the scene’s environment and bounces (the ground under a foot), and carrying that up with a lifted limb amounts to rotating a lightmap. A skinned cloud carries its material and is relit every frame. Its light transfer, when one is asked for, is kept in a form that rotates with the gaussian (§5.7).

5.3 Influences per gaussian, and their gradients

mesh_pack.slang writes each triangle corner’s four heaviest influences into a second picture laid out exactly like the mesh’s, so the AOFX effect addresses both the same way. The effect knows where inside the triangle each gaussian stands, so it blends the corners there (blendInfluences, mesh2splat.slang). With barycentric coordinates λa,λb,λc\lambda_a,\lambda_b,\lambda_c and corner weights wcjw_{cj}, the raw weight of joint jj is

w̃j=∑c∈{a,b,c}wcjλc.\tilde w_j = \sum_{c\in\{a,b,c\}} w_{cj}\,\lambda_c .

Twelve (joint, weight) pairs go in and four come out. A joint already present gains the weight, an empty slot takes a new joint, and otherwise the new joint replaces the lightest. Once the four joints are chosen, each kept weight is recomputed as its full blend and the four are renormalised, wk=w̃k/Ww_k = \tilde w_k / W with W=∑i∈Kw̃iW=\sum_{i\in K}\tilde w_i. A gaussian is therefore always fully carried.

Gradients. w̃j\tilde w_j is linear on the triangle, with ∇w̃j=∑cwcj∇λc\nabla\tilde w_j=\sum_c w_{cj}\nabla\lambda_c, where ∇λb=(e2×n)/|n|2\nabla\lambda_b = (e_2\times n)/|n|^2, ∇λc=(n×e1)/|n|2\nabla\lambda_c = (n\times e_1)/|n|^2 and ∇λa=−∇λb−∇λc\nabla\lambda_a = -\nabla\lambda_b-\nabla\lambda_c. The gradient of the renormalised weight follows from the quotient rule:

∇wk=∇w̃k−wk∑i∈K∇w̃iW.\nabla w_k = \frac{\nabla\tilde w_k - w_k\sum_{i\in K}\nabla\tilde w_i}{W}.

This is exact at the gaussian’s centre. Only the two components along the gaussian’s rest axes (û,v̂)(\hat u,\hat v) act on a disc, and the gradients sum to zero, so only the first three joints’ gradients are stored. That is three words of two halves, 12 bytes per gaussian, in primvars:athenea:splat:jointWeightGradients (half[], elementSize 6). The fourth gradient is minus the sum of the other three. With gradients, the effect’s record for a carried gaussian grows to nine entries.

Measured. A square bound entirely to one joint converts to 1056 gaussians, all naming that joint with full weight. Through the effect, 272 gaussians of a quad whose corners all name joint 3 come out on joint 3, with the other slots empty and the weights summing to one. With one joint everywhere, the stored gradient is below 10−310^{-3}.

Truncation. Four influences is a truncation. On the sparrow, 27 % of wing vertices carry more than four (6686 vertices, elementSize 25), and a gaussian takes the union of three corners’ joints, so the share of truncated gaussians is at least as large. That share has not been measured.

5.4 Skinning a Gaussian on the device

scene::SplatSkinner dispatches shaders/athenea/scene/splat_skin.slang, one thread per gaussian. Its inputs are the rest cloud, the (joint, weight) pairs, the joints’ skinning transforms at the current instant, and three matrices: the bind transform GG, skelToWorld and worldToPrim. USD matrices are row-major and act on row vectors, so the host transposes them as geom::Skinner does for meshes.

5.4.1 The mean

Let q=Gpq = G\,p be the rest point in bind space, Xjq=Ajq+tjX_j q = A_j q + t_j joint jj’s skinning transform, and M=𝚠𝚘𝚛𝚕𝚍𝚃𝚘𝙿𝚛𝚒𝚖⋅𝚜𝚔𝚎𝚕𝚃𝚘𝚆𝚘𝚛𝚕𝚍M=\texttt{worldToPrim}\cdot\texttt{skelToWorld}. The posed mean is linear blend skinning, with exactly the arithmetic the mesh skinner uses:

μ′=M∑jwjXj(Gμ).\mu' = M \sum_j w_j\,X_j\,(G\,\mu).

5.4.2 The frame: the whole Jacobian

The posed covariance should be the rest covariance pushed through the derivative of the position map. When the weights vary in space, that derivative is

J=L(M)[∑jwjAjL(G)+∑j(Xjq)∇pwj𝖳],J = L(M)\Big[\sum_j w_j A_j\,L(G) \;+\; \sum_j (X_j q)\,\nabla_p w_j^{\mathsf T}\Big],

where L(⋅)L(\cdot) is the linear part. The first term is the usual “blend the joints’ linear parts”. The second, the elastic term, is what stretches a gaussian across a bend where two joints disagree about where its point goes. With two joints a relative angle θ\theta apart and a weight transition of length LL, its size relative to the first term is about 2tan⁡(θ/2)d/L2\tan(\theta/2)\,d/L, which is of order one at a right angle.

The kernel builds the elastic term in the same loop that forms XkqX_k q. It accumulates ∑k(Xkq)gk\sum_k (X_k q)\,g_k on each rest axis, relative to the blended point. The gradients sum to zero, so this is the same sum, but it is computed as a difference of nearby points, which stays exact in float far from the origin. A joint with zero weight but a non-zero gradient still contributes.

The posed covariance in the gaussian’s plane is

Σ′=JES2E𝖳J𝖳,E=[ûv̂],S=diag(su,sv).\Sigma' = J\,E\,S^2E^{\mathsf T}J^{\mathsf T},\qquad E=[\hat u\ \hat v],\ S=\mathrm{diag}(s_u,s_v).

The kernel takes an orthonormal basis (e1,e2)(e_1,e_2) of the plane spanned by a=Jûsua=J\hat u\,s_u and b=Jv̂svb=J\hat v\,s_v, writes [ab]=[e1e2]K[a\ b]=[e_1\ e_2]K with KK upper triangular, and diagonalises C=KK𝖳C=KK^{\mathsf T} in closed form. The larger eigenvalue is m+rm+r, with m=12(c00+c11)m=\tfrac12(c_{00}+c_{11}) and r=14(c00−c11)2+c012r=\sqrt{\tfrac14(c_{00}-c_{11})^2+c_{01}^2}. The smaller is computed as (k00k11)2/(m+r)(k_{00}k_{11})^2/(m+r) rather than m−rm-r, which would cancel to nothing for a thin gaussian in float. The new axes are the eigenvectors, the new sizes the square roots of the eigenvalues. The first axis is the eigenvector closer to the first carried axis, and where c01c_{01} is pure rounding the basis is kept exactly, so a round gaussian is not spun. The third axis is the plane’s normal, and the third size (a disc’s thickness) is left as converted. The shading normal turns with the cofactor of the whole JJ, because (Ja)×(Jb)=cof(J)(a×b)(Ja)\times(Jb)=\mathrm{cof}(J)(a\times b).

The kernel writes only position, rotation and the two in-plane scales. Opacity, colour, harmonics, PBR channels and the word holding the third size are left untouched. A frame therefore costs one kernel over the cloud and nothing is decoded again.

5.5 The file: the rig as USD

A skinned cloud is a ParticleField3DGaussianSplat. The rig was first written under AtheneaSplatSkinningAPI, and is now written under UsdSkel’s own names. mesh2splat --skinned applies SkelBindingAPI and writes skel:jointIndices and skel:jointWeights (elementSize 4), skel:geomBindTransform, skel:joints and skel:skeleton. It also writes primvars:athenea:splat:skinningXforms: one matrix per joint, in skeleton order, time sampled. This is the only property of an animated cloud that changes from frame to frame. The transforms are UsdSkelSkeletonQuery::ComputeSkinningTransforms at each instant of --range START:END[:STEP].

Binding to the Skeleton. usdSkelImaging builds ext computations only for meshes, curves and points. It does, however, resolve any Skeleton’s skinningTransforms at any instant. 5.6 The engine: posing, bounds, refit and motion blur

Engine::carryCloud pairs the file’s two arrays into the (joint, weight) layout, keeps the uploaded cloud as the bind pose, and runs the skinner into a separate GpuSplats that shares the harmonics and PBR buffers, since the skinner writes neither. Measuring this exposed two errors. First, a posed cloud’s extent is not the bind pose’s, so the extent is now folded again on the device after skinning. Second, Engine::bounds() folded the uploaded cloud instead of the drawn one.

A time step is not a new cloud. At first, every time-code change re-decoded every array and rebuilt the CPU-side influence pairs (137 MB on the 4.27 M-gaussian sparrow). Arrays are now compared by identity (skinningXforms excluded), pairs are rebuilt only on upload, and arrays with no time samples are held (_held), since the crate file’s compressed integer arrays arrive in new buffers on every read. Motion blur from the skeleton. ParticleField samples skinningXforms over the shutter. The skinner blends the second pose in the same loop (points only, about a third of a kernel) and writes each gaussian’s displacement as two words of halves. The rasteriser turns that displacement into a rank-one covariance term. Hydra brackets rather than clips the shutter, so the displacement is scaled by (tc−to)/(t1−t0)(t_c-t_o)/(t_1-t_0); without that factor the wings blur twice as far. On the 5.9 M sparrow at 1280×800, a frame takes 90.0 ms with shutter 0 and 100.5 ms with shutter 0.5 (+11 %).

5.7 Light that turns with a limb: zonal transfer lobes

A static cloud can carry a precomputed transfer of nine spherical harmonics in world space. Under a skeleton those harmonics would keep the sky of the conversion pose, and rotating them costs a 9×99\times9 matrix per gaussian. athenea instead stores one or two zonal lobes per gaussian, with each lobe’s axis expressed in the gaussian’s own frame. A zonal function rotates by rotating its axis, a single 3×33\times3, so whatever frame the skinner produces carries the lobes with it, and no gaussian has to be assigned to a part.

A lobe with axis aa and coefficients zlz_l is, in harmonics, glm=zlklYlm(a)g_{lm}=z_l\,k_l\,Y_{lm}(a) with kl=4π/(2l+1)k_l=\sqrt{4\pi/(2l+1)}. For a fixed axis the best coefficients are the projection zl=kl∑mflmYlm(a)z_l = k_l\sum_m f_{lm}Y_{lm}(a). By the addition theorem the residual is

∥f−g∥2=∥f∥2−∑l=02zl2,\|f-g\|^2=\|f\|^2-\sum_{l=0}^{2} z_l^2,

so the axis is chosen to maximise ∑lzl2\sum_l z_l^2. On the device the search tries the band-1 direction, the normal, and 64 Fibonacci directions on a hemisphere, then refines by a walk that halves its step. The second lobe is fitted to the first lobe’s residual, and the two are refitted against each other twice (Sloan’s ZH fit). The data is ten floats per gaussian, primvars:athenea:splat:transferZonal (elementSize 10): per lobe, the octahedral (u,v)(u,v) of the axis in the frame and z0,z1,z2z_0,z_1,z_2. On the device it is the ordinary transfer buffer with transferCount = 10. At read time, splatTransferFrame turns each axis into world space using the gaussian’s current rotation and rebuilds the nine harmonics.

The bake pose. At first the bake posed the cloud at --time using four influences per gaussian, while Hydra skinned the mesh with all of its own (ten per vertex on the feathers, 25 on the wings). Where the two disagreed, the bake’s ray missed its surface, and 566 603 of 1 504 160 gaussians came out transparent. The bake now holds the skeleton in its bind pose (StageRenderer::holdBindPose) and poses the cloud with identity transforms: 1 504 156 of 1 504 160 find their surface. The transfer therefore holds the bind pose’s self-shadowing (wings spread). Occlusion that another limb casts in another pose is the job of §5.8.

Checked. In [zonal], a two-lobe transfer survives the fit and f16 storage within 0.5 %. Turning a gaussian’s frame by RR and reading along RwRw gives what the unturned gaussian gives along ww. In a USD test, 512 gaussians carried by a joint that turns 60° match the same cloud under an Xform of that rotation, with relMSE below 10−410^{-4} on both raster and traced routes. The fit’s error on the sparrow has not been measured. The indirect half of the transfer is not kept zonal.

5.8 Per-part visibility fields: baked once, a product of table reads per frame

A relit cloud also has to shadow itself, and the measurements ruled out the obvious routes. In the traced route --splat-shadows changed nothing, because a traced splat had no shadow pass. In the raster route it cost 36× the frame (49.58 → 1810.24 ms) and crushed the bird to black. Sweeping the self-offset from 1 to 800 own sigmas moved the mean image monotonically from 0 % to 99 % of the unshadowed value, with no plateau. The literature agrees that baked visibility does not survive articulation unless it is factored by body part: Lin et al. use directional MLPs per part, and DNF-Avatar a scalar AO grid per bone. athenea combines the two ideas: a directional table per part.

Partition. partitionJoints takes the largest subtree not yet a part, with at least minJoints (6) joints, and makes it a part, until there are --parts (12). On the sparrow this gives root, centre, chest, pelvis, head, and each wing in three segments. Each gaussian belongs to the part of its dominant joint.

Bake (athenea visibility, splat_visibility_bake.slang). This is done once, in the bind pose. For part pp, a grid of N3N^3 probes covers the cloud’s box plus a 5 % margin. Each probe holds an octahedral map of O2O^2 directions. A texel stores the transmittance of a ray leaving the probe through part pp’s gaussians alone. The part’s gaussians are gathered into a cloud of their own and traced with inline rays, using the same product of (1−α)(1-\alpha) that the shadow kernels use. Each probe also stores an ambient term, its mean over directions, used for domes.

Read (splat_visibility_read.slang). Every frame, visPartFrames computes one affine map per part from the posed space back to the bind space where the field lives:

xrest=UAkp−1(x′−tkp)−Ug,U=L(G)−1,x_{\text{rest}} = U A_{k_p}^{-1}\,(x' - t_{k_p}) - U g,\qquad U=L(G)^{-1},

where kpk_p is the part’s head joint, Xkp=(Akp,tkp)X_{k_p}=(A_{k_p},t_{k_p}) its skinning transform and gg the translation of GG; call this map Tp−1T_p^{-1}. Before this map was precomputed it cost two 3×33\times3 inverses per gaussian per part per light, about 260 million a frame on the sparrow; precomputing it took the read from 12.7 to 8.0 ms (5 887 323 gaussians, 1600×1000). The factor for gaussian ii and light direction ωℓ\omega_\ell is then the product over the other parts:

Viℓ=∏p≠π(i)Fp(Tp−1xi,L(Tp)−1ωℓ),V_{i\ell}=\prod_{p\ne \pi(i)} F_p\!\left(T_p^{-1}x_i,\;L(T_p)^{-1}\omega_\ell\right),

where π(i)\pi(i) is the gaussian’s own part, FpF_p is read trilinearly over the probes and bilinearly over the octahedral map, and is 1 outside the part’s box. A dome uses the ambient term instead. The factors go into the same shadowFactors that both routes read, so the traced route, which had no splat shadows at all, gets them too. No ray is traced per frame.

Found while building it. An empty part’s unwritten texels read as total shadow and turned the bird black; a grid of 0 now marks it and it is skipped. The bake traces inline rays, which the CUDA target lacks, so it is a separate module and CUDA devices read fields baked elsewhere. A part does not shadow itself: its field, read at its own gaussian, is the crowd it stands in. 5.9 Levels of detail and decimation for skinned clouds

The .athc octree merges Morton-ordered cells. That cannot apply to a skinned cloud, because a cell that took wing and body together would not know which joint to follow. Two mechanisms replace it.

Discrete levels by reconversion. mesh2splat --lod-levels N converts at the requested resolution, then at half of it, and so on, each level a cloud with its own rig. Engine::lodLevelsFor draws the coarsest level whose cell spans no more than athenea:lod:threshold pixels (default 1) at the nearest point of its posed bounds. Undrawn levels are not posed (deferredPose). Sparrow clips at 1100 / 550 / 275 cells (5.9 M / 1.5 M / 0.4 M gaussians), 1600×1000: 49.6 → 51.5 ms when the bird fills the frame; 71.3 → 24.7 ms at four times the distance. Every level stays resident with its own visibility fields (81 MB each), and switching between levels is a step, not a blend.

Decimation that keeps the rig. usd::decimateStage finds every per-gaussian array and merges each by kind (lod_attributes.slang). When skel:jointIndices has a matching skel:jointWeights, the pair is merged as a rig (mode 3). Over the run of records RR that a kept gaussian stands for, each with merge weight ωr\omega_r,

cj=∑r∈Rωr∑kwrk[jrk=j],c_j=\sum_{r\in R}\omega_r\sum_k w_{rk}\,[\,j_{rk}=j\,],

collecting up to 16 distinct joints. The width heaviest are kept and renormalised, wj′=cj/∑keptcw'_j = c_j/\sum_{\text{kept}} c. A record’s dominant joint is also folded into the merge key, so a merge never crosses a joint’s edge. Checked: a floor whose halves belong to two joints goes from 4096 to 256 gaussians, each kept gaussian wholly on its side’s joint. The sparrow’s finest level (5 887 323 gaussians, with ids, material, visibility parts and rig) keeps 87.7 % in 28 s. Posed at time 20, it draws as the undecimated cloud at an image RMS of 0.002.

5.13 What is not done

  • More than four influences per gaussian (P014C). This limits accuracy at the sparrow’s wing tips, both in the animation and in the bake’s agreement with the mesh.
  • Dual-quaternion skinning for clouds. Retargeting and clip blending, which a cloud holding baked transforms cannot do.
  • Several skinned meshes at different prim transforms (only the first one’s is kept), and skeletons with their own animated transform.
  • The traced route poses once per frame, so it does not blur a skinned cloud. LoD instances carry no motion.
  • The part’s own self-shadowing term. Cross-part occlusion in poses other than the bind pose, inside the zonal transfer. Visibility bakes beyond about 3 M gaussians per part.
  • The zonal fit’s error on the sparrow, and a geometric measure of coverage at a close bend, which is what would show the gain of the elastic Jacobian term.

6. Levels of detail: merge trees, cuts by error, pages and occlusion

A cloud baked from a mesh at 1280 px holds 8.7 million Gaussians for one car. The close-up level baked at 5120 px holds 74.5 million. The phone’s budget, set by the user, is 50–60 MB resident and about 250 k Gaussians drawn at 30 fps. Every figure between those limits depends on the level-of-detail structure. This section first describes the engine’s original level of detail (§6.1), which was built for captured scenes, and explains why it does not meet that budget. It then covers what replaced it in the pieces work: per-part pair-merge trees (§6.2), one cut by a bounded screen error (§6.3), the opacity a merge is given (§6.4) and a failed attempt to improve it (§6.5), two view-dependent culls (§6.6, §6.7), pages ordered by error (§6.8), the 5120 px close-up level (§6.9) and the measurements (§6.10). Every kernel named here is a Slang compute kernel. The CPU reads files and does bookkeeping only (§2).

Notation. A node nn is a leaf (a baked Gaussian) or a merge. μn\mu_n is its centre and Σn\Sigma_n its covariance, with scales sn=(sx,sy,sz)s_n = (s_x, s_y, s_z) and opacity ono_n. par(n)\mathrm{par}(n) is its parent. τ\tau is the threshold in pixels, ff is the focal length in pixels at unit distance (pixelsPerUnit), and ee is the eye.

6.1 The engine’s original level of detail

Octree by Morton code. LodBuilder (modules/lod, shaders/athenea/lod/lod_common.slang) sorts the splats by a 30-bit Morton code. The octree cell of a splat at level rr is the top 3r3r bits of its code, so the cells of a level are runs of equal prefixes. A boundary pass and a prefix sum find them, and no tree is walked. Each cell carries moments, following Kerbl et al. 2024. With w=o⋅A2(s)w = o \cdot A_2(s), where A2A_2 is the area of the two longest axes,

W=∑w,S1=∑wμ,S2=∑w(Σ+μμ⊤),C=∑wc.W=\sum w,\quad S_1=\sum w\,\mu,\quad S_2=\sum w\,(\Sigma+\mu\mu^\top),\quad C=\sum w\,c .

Moments add, so each coarser level is summed from its children. A cell becomes one Gaussian with μ=S1/W\mu=S_1/W, Σ=S2/W−μμ⊤\Sigma=S_2/W-\mu\mu^\top (Jacobi sweeps in the shader), c=C/Wc=C/W and o=min⁡(W/A2,0.99)o=\min(W/A_2,0.99). The stored levels run from coarsestLevel down to the deepest level whose cell count is at most half the splat count.

Cut. CutSelector draws a group when its cell’s edge, projected at the cell’s nearest distance, is at most τ\tau and its parent cell’s projection is above τ\tau. A child cell lies inside its parent, so the test is monotone along any root-to-leaf line, and every place is drawn at exactly one level. The only readback is a count per level and a read per instance per frame.

Chunks, .athc and streaming. The splats are cut into chunks of 65 536 consecutive Morton-ordered splats. The merged levels stay on the device, and the chunks are streamed. The .athc format (.lrtc in the lucabRTrender tree) has a 104 B header, level and chunk tables, and blocks aligned to 4 KiB so that a chunk can be mapped and faulted in alone. StreamingPool maps the file. Two loader threads copy the wanted chunks, most wanted first. Each slot is taken from a free slot, from the chunk wanted least recently, or from a chunk wanted less than half as much. A finest-level group whose chunks are missing draws its own merged Gaussian, so the image never has a hole. On train_7k (742 k splats, M5 Pro), the build took 47 ms and produced 146 k merged Gaussians. At 8 px the cut drew 83 k splats (11 %), rendering in 3.3 ms against 12.6 ms, with a mean absolute error of 3⋅10−33\cdot10^{-3}. On train_30k, the .athc streamed into 4 of 16 slots wrote byte-identical EXRs and settled in 2 cuts.

Why it was replaced for the phone. The structure was mapped on 10 Oct (PIECES-PLAN.md), and four properties did not fit the goal.

  1. The threshold is set per instance and is not a budget. Each instance costs two blocking submits and a readback per frame, and no budget is shared across instances.
  2. The octree cells ignore the object. A cell merges whatever falls inside it, including a badge with the panel beneath it and glass with paint.
  3. A streamed splat costs 132 B, against the ~55 B target.
  4. Only the splats are chunked. The merged levels, down to one with up to half as many cells as splats, are always resident, and for millions of leaves they alone exceed the budget.

6.2 Per-part pair-merge trees

athenea decimate --tree builds a binary merge tree by mutual nearest-neighbour pair merging. It is NanoGS (Xiong et al. 2026) on the device (lod_pairs.slang, lod_pairs_final.slang, Lod.cpp), with guards that suit baked material clouds.

6.2.1 Nodes as moments

A node is a vector of 24 floats: WW, WμW\mu, W(Σ+μμ⊤)W(\Sigma+\mu\mu^\top) (6), W⋅sRGB(c)W\cdot\mathrm{sRGB}(c), WnW n, WαW\alpha, W⋅W\cdotmetallic, W⋅W\cdottransmission, W⋅W\cdotspecular, the key, the record count, the weighted thickness ratio and the node’s index in the tree. Here w=o⋅max⁡(sxsy,sysz,sxsz)w = o\cdot\max(s_xs_y, s_ys_z, s_xs_z) (discArea), and α\alpha is the GGX roughness squared. A merge sums the two vectors. Because sums are associative, a node’s moments are those of all its leaves whatever the merge order. Every other attribute (transfer, shadow bits, material, joints) is merged once at the end, mass-weighted over the node’s run of leaves (lodMergeAttribute).

The merged Gaussian (pairFinalize) takes the moment-matched μ\mu and Σ\Sigma, eigen-decomposed by Jacobi with the thinnest axis third. Merging by moments preserves the second moment. A 2×22\times2 block of discs as wide as their spacing therefore merges into one disc 0.58 of the spacing wide, which leaves visible holes when seen up close. Along each wide axis uu, the merge is widened as follows. Let vuv_u be the area-weighted variance of the member centres, cuc_u their mean width, and d2=12vuvv/(n−1)d^2 = 12\sqrt{v_uv_v}/(n-1) the spacing of a grid of nn members over that spread. Then

su′=clamp(cud12vu+d2,su,2su).s_u' = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{c_u}{d}\sqrt{12v_u+d^2},\; s_u,\; 2s_u\right).

The opacity under the default rule is the members’ area-weighted mean, capped at 0.99. The mass coverage W/A(s′)W/A(s') is kept as coverageMass. A merge of discs whose thinnest axis is under 0.12 of its middle axis is written as a surfel (thinnest scale exactly 0).

6.2.2 The cost

For nodes a,ba, b with π=Wa/(Wa+Wb)\pi = W_a/(W_a+W_b) and merged Σ\Sigma, the shape term is Runnalls’ KL bound per unit mass. The disc’s area replaces the volume, which a surfel does not have:

cshape=max⁡(0,12[log⁡I2(Σ)−πlog⁡I2(Σa)−(1−π)log⁡I2(Σb)]),c_{\text{shape}} = \max\!\Big(0,\ \tfrac12\big[\log I_2(\Sigma) - \pi\log I_2(\Sigma_a) - (1-\pi)\log I_2(\Sigma_b)\big]\Big),

where I2I_2 is the second invariant (sum of pairwise eigenvalue products, computed without eigenvalues). The full cost is

c=σpx(cshape+wfϕ2+wn(1−cos⁡θab)(1+F0/α))+wc‖ΔcsRGB‖2+wmΔmat,c = \sigma_{\text{px}}\big(c_{\text{shape}} + w_f\,\phi^2 + w_n(1-\cos\theta_{ab})(1+F_0/\alpha)\big) + w_c\,\lVert\Delta c_{\text{sRGB}}\rVert^2 + w_m\,\Delta_{\text{mat}},

where ϕ\phi is the thickness the merge adds over its thicker member. The term σpx=smoothstep(0.5,2,px)\sigma_{\text{px}} = \mathrm{smoothstep}(0.5, 2, \text{px}) removes the shape terms of merges that are under half a pixel on the conversion camera. Defaults: wc=20w_c=20, wn=1w_n=1, wm=4w_m=4, wf=4w_f=4.

6.2.4 Passes and the relaxed top

In each pass, every node computes the cost of merging with the 8 neighbours on either side along a Morton order of the centres. It then repeats this along a second order shifted by half the finest cell, and keeps the cheaper partner. Pairs that chose each other are binned into a log⁡10\log_{10} cost histogram, and the host picks the cost threshold that merges as many pairs as the pass may, which is at most half the nodes. Merging stops at treeRatio (4096×) or when nothing more may merge. With relaxTop, merging then continues with the colour edge (2.0), the angle, the glass-among-itself guard, the aspect and the pixel limit lifted. The key, glass-with-non-glass and mirror guards stay. Each such merge records its cost negated, which marks it as past the guards. This gives a far view coarse levels to cut to: one headlight cover went from 76 486 roots to 123 (3d7ee66).

Layout. The tree is written as a ParticleField. The leaves come first, in depth-first order, so that every node is a contiguous run of leaves. The merges follow in the order they were made. Each node carries lodParent (a root is its own parent), lodStart (its first leaf), lodSize (its number of leaves) and lodCost.

Merges never cross parts, so each group’s tree is exactly the subtree the whole asset would hold for those parts. 6.3 The cut by error, with bounds

The first cut (--cut-pixels, 655c027) kept a node when its on-screen width 2max⁡(s)f/d2\max(s)f/d was at most τ\tau and no ancestor’s was. Merges past the guards passed only at 0.25τ0.25\tau. That cut is still available, but an error cut replaced it (2bb1b80, e13b08b; research 100, after Nanite).

Own error. Let rn=‖sn‖r_n=\lVert s_n\rVert (the square root of trΣn\mathrm{tr}\,\Sigma_n) and αn=max⁡(roughness,0.02)2\alpha_n = \max(\text{roughness},0.02)^2. A merge’s own error in metres is

ε0(n)=rn(|costn|+wcsc(n)+wnθ(n)2αn+wsmax⁡(En−E0,0)),wc=10,wn=1,\varepsilon_0(n) = r_n\Big(|\mathrm{cost}_n| + w_c\,s_c(n) + w_n\,\frac{\theta(n)^2}{\alpha_n} + w_s\max(E_n-E_0,0)\Big),\qquad w_c=10,\ w_n=1,

and ε0=0\varepsilon_0=0 for a leaf. Merges past the guards (costn<0\mathrm{cost}_n<0) are not measured by their cost, which misses the moiré it causes on glass and the colour it drags across an edge. They are therefore held to

ε0(n)←max⁡(gε0(n),2rn),g=4(–𝚌𝚞𝚝-𝚐𝚞𝚊𝚛𝚍).\varepsilon_0(n) \leftarrow \max\big(g\,\varepsilon_0(n),\ 2r_n\big),\qquad g=4\ (\texttt{--cut-guard}).

The spill term wsw_s is 0 by default (§6.5).

Bounds. A merge’s cost covers only the last pair it joined. A chain of merges, each within the colour edge, can drift across that edge. So a colour spread scs_c and a normal cone θ\theta are climbed up the tree by the triangle inequality (treeBoundsUp), starting at 0 for the leaves:

sc(p)=maxk∈ch(p)(sc(k)+‖ck−cp‖),θ(p)=maxk(θ(k)+∠(nk,np)).s_c(p) = \max_{k\in\mathrm{ch}(p)}\big(s_c(k)+\lVert c_k-c_p\rVert\big),\qquad \theta(p) = \max_{k}\big(\theta(k)+\angle(n_k,n_p)\big).

Both are therefore bounds on every leaf under pp.

Monotone error and covering spheres. The error is made monotone, and every node gets a sphere that contains its children’s spheres:

ε(n)=max⁡(ε0(n),maxkε(k)),ρ(n)=maxk(‖μn−μk‖+ρ(k)),ρ(leaf)=2max⁡(s).\varepsilon(n) = \max\big(\varepsilon_0(n),\ \max_{k}\varepsilon(k)\big),\qquad \rho(n)=\max_k\big(\lVert\mu_n-\mu_k\rVert+\rho(k)\big),\quad \rho(\text{leaf})=2\max(s).

Each climb is one kernel launched over all nodes, in which every node raises its parent with InterlockedMax on the float’s bits. The kernel is relaunched until a counter reads zero, which is the tree’s depth in rounds. The error is projected at the sphere’s nearest point:

P(n)=ε(n)fmax⁡(‖μn−e‖−ρ(n),10−3).P(n) = \frac{\varepsilon(n)\,f}{\max(\lVert\mu_n-e\rVert-\rho(n),\ 10^{-3})}.

Proposition. If P(n)≤τ<P(par(n))P(n)\le\tau<P(\mathrm{par}(n)) selects nn (a root needs only the first inequality), then every leaf is drawn exactly once. Proof. ε\varepsilon does not decrease going up. The parent’s sphere contains the child’s, so the distance to the sphere decreases going up. PP therefore does not decrease along any leaf-to-root path. Leaves have P=0≤τP=0\le\tau, so the predicate “P≤τP\le\tau” holds on a prefix of the path starting at the leaf, and exactly one node is the last of that prefix. ▫\square

So the cut is a single parallel test per node (treeErrorKeep), with no walk up the ancestors. --cut-budget N bisects log⁡τ\log\tau over [10−4,104][10^{-4},10^4] in 40 steps for the smallest threshold whose cut holds at most NN nodes.

6.4 The opacity of a merge: least-squares coverage

Mass over the moment ellipse counts the leaves’ overlapping tails twice. Research measured the effect at 27 % at the median and 2.3× at p10. On the grille at 393 px, merges drew darker than their leaves. --cut-coverage (0ab577e) and then --tree-coverage (73c0ec2, f980e42) set every merge’s opacity from what its leaves actually cover, as follows.

  1. The node’s plane is spanned by its two widest axes u,vu,v, with scales su,svs_u,s_v. An 8×88\times8 grid covers the core [−1.25,1.25]2[-1.25,1.25]^2 in units of (su,sv)(s_u,s_v).
  2. Each leaf ll under the node (its run, up to 4096 leaves when writing the tree and 2048 in a cut) is projected onto the plane. Its in-plane covariance is (u⊤Σlu,u⊤Σlv,v⊤Σlv)(u^\top\Sigma_lu,\ u^\top\Sigma_lv,\ v^\top\Sigma_lv) and its centre offset is mlm_l. A longer run keeps its mass-based opacity. 6.5 The spill metric, and why it failed for the grille

At a coarse cut the grille’s holes closed: in T66 the near-ground region read 2.24× the path trace in the 0.25 px cut, against 1.18 in the leaves. 6.6 Back-face culling of closed shells

At the phone’s size, a cut showed back faces as black. On the grille at 393 px, culling back faces raised the uncut leaves from 38.2 to 41.6 dB (research). --cut-cull-back drops a node whose whole normal cone faces away:

θ(n)<π2∧nn⋅e−μn‖e−μn‖<−sin⁡θ(n).\theta(n)<\tfrac{\pi}{2}\ \wedge\ n_n\cdot\frac{e-\mu_n}{\lVert e-\mu_n\rVert} < -\sin\theta(n).

The cone bounds every leaf under the node (§6.3), so the node’s whole subtree faces away too. Two observations set where it applies. Applied to the six test pieces with the headlight’s glass cover among them, it cost 6 dB at 1920: glass is seen through, so its back faces are visible. On the whole car at 393 px (T50), the doors and panels, which are two skins, showed a net of dark cracks: the inner skin’s merges, facing away and lit by the dark interior, sorted in front of the outer skin in patches (T51: the same with or without coverage; --cut-guard 1000 changed nothing). On a closed shell, what lies behind a face that looks away is the part’s own inside, so culling it is safe and removes the net (T56). The cull is therefore applied, to leaves and merges alike, only on closed shells that are not glass (primvars:athenea:splat:closed, transmission ≤0.5\le 0.5).

The open-edge rule. mesh2splat counts the edges not shared by exactly two triangles, OO. It first marked a mesh closed when O=0O=0 (40a7927) and then under 2 % of its edges (2b05ebb, 111 of 192 meshes). An open sheet’s border grows as the square root of its area, so a fine tube passed the share rule: a rim’s barrel at 1 % was culled, and light showed between the spokes on the phone. The rule kept is

closed⇔O<2E,E=1.5T,\text{closed} \iff O < 2\sqrt{E},\qquad E = 1.5\,T,

for a mesh of TT triangles (f52b6b4). Under it, doors (0), logos (1.4) and calipers (1.5) are closed, and rims (4.0), tyres (3.1), the hood (3.1) and the bumper (4.4) are not, which gives 102 of the Corvette’s 192 meshes. 6.7 Occlusion masks

A TX transfer stores 256 shadow bits per Gaussian, one per cell of a 16×1616\times16 octahedral grid over the world’s sphere, set where a ray from the Gaussian reached the sky. A direction whose cells are all closed is one from which an eye outside the asset cannot see the Gaussian. The shading bits cannot be reused for this: a merge holds its leaves’ bits by majority, and the phone’s codebook (shadow_vq) is also a majority, so both would close directions from which some leaf is seen. --occlusion-mask N (1000bdf, occlusion_mask.slang, Occlusion.cpp) builds a conservative mask per node.

Masks. A leaf’s mask MlM_l is its open cells dilated by one cell (3×33\times3), mirrored across the octahedral square’s edge at the fold. One cell is ~11°, and without the margin an occluder’s edge closed directions from which a grille’s chrome was still visible. The mask is all open on glass (transmission >0.5>0.5, since what lies behind glass is seen through it) and on a leaf with no bits. A merge’s mask is the OR of its children’s, climbed until no mask changes:

Mp=⋁k∈ch(p)Mk.M_p = \bigvee_{k\in\mathrm{ch}(p)} M_k .

Conservative codebook. The masks are replaced by an index into NN entries of 256 bits each. Entry 0 is all open. A node’s worth is the number of closed cells among the relevant ones, the directions with d⋅up̂>−0.25d\cdot\hat{up} > -0.25 (an eye down to ~15° below the horizon). The nodes in the lowest log⁡2\log_2-worth bins, together under 2 % of the total worth, take entry 0 and stay out of the fit. The remaining entries are seeded from fitted nodes spread through the file. Round 1 assigns each node to the entry nearest in Hamming distance over the relevant cells. Rounds 2–8 assign a node only to an entry that contains its open set, choosing the one with the fewest relevant open cells. After each round an entry becomes the OR of its members. Every entry is therefore a superset of each member’s open set, and the codebook can never close a direction a node has open. The codebook’s cost is the cells it reopens (Mentry∧¬MnM_{\text{entry}}\wedge\lnot M_n) as a share of all closed cells.

Shared by need. One asset’s trees share one codebook (--occlusion-group, 5be8710). Each unit is fitted alone at the full size and the entries it uses are counted. The codebook is then shared out as shareu=16+⌊spare⋅usedu/∑used⌋\text{share}_u = 16 + \lfloor\text{spare}\cdot \text{used}_u/\sum\text{used}\rfloor, and each unit is refitted within its share (1103f9f). 6.8 Pages by error

A player cannot hold a whole tree. It needs units that it fetches whole and tests without reading their nodes.

By level fails. Pages taken as breadth-first tops of sibling subtrees still needed 114 of the hood’s 263 pages for a far cut. The bounds keep small nodes wherever a highlight or a colour turns, so the cut spans nodes from single leaves to 2112^{11} leaves (e562899).

By error works. A node is needed exactly when it is a root or its parent’s projected error exceeds τ\tau. So the layout follows the parent’s error (lod_pages.slang). The band of a node is

b(n)=clamp(⌊log⁡2ε(par(n))⌋+40,0,63),b(root)=63.b(n) = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\lfloor\log_2\varepsilon(\mathrm{par}(n))\rfloor+40,\ 0,\ 63\big),\qquad b(\text{root}) = 63 .

The sort key, in the pages’ own order, is (coarsest band first, then first leaf, then larger node first):

hi=(63−b)≪22|bucket≪19|start≫6,lo=(start&63)≪26|(226−1−leaves).\text{hi} = (63-b)\ll22 \;|\; \text{bucket}\ll19 \;|\; \text{start}\gg6,\qquad \text{lo} = (\text{start}\,\&\,63)\ll26 \;|\; (2^{26}-1-\text{leaves}).

A GPU radix sort orders the nodes, and page pp holds sorted positions [pP,(p+1)P)[pP,(p+1)P). The pages cross band boundaries, so a coarse band of a few nodes does not become a page of its own. A parent’s band is never finer than its child’s, its first leaf is never later, and when both tie the parent is the larger node. A parent is therefore never placed after its child. An earlier layout ran each band’s leaves backwards and put 92 parents after their children on the hood (e2c2046). --tree-pages P writes, for each node, lodPage, and for each page:

  • lodPageInfo = (coarsest band, nodes, first node in page order), and
  • lodPageBounds = the box holding the parents’ spheres (a node’s need is measured from its parent) and the largest and smallest parent error.

The player projects the largest error from the box and loads the page when that projection exceeds τ\tau. The bucket bits (--pages-by-facing: dominant normal axis and sign) made residency worse, 4.45× against 3.98× at τ=2.5\tau=2.5, and are off.

6.11 Limits and open items

  • Projection-only holes. A coarse cut of sub-pixel strips (the grille) fills holes that no per-node, view-free measure can see (§6.5). This is resolved in practice by finer leaves, not by the cut.
  • Occlusion weighting. The codebook is shared by entries used, not by how often a view culls a node. Weighting by an orbit of views in the bake should lift culling past 84 % of the ideal.
  • Residency at ~4×. It is bound by pages that mix needed and view-culled nodes. The phone splits traversal from payload rather than shrinking pages.
  • Glass merges. Every thin-sheet Gaussian is lifted to the 0.1 sheet floor, so merging fewer, larger sheets varies the transmission cell to cell. Spark declares sheet merges at 4× their size in the interim, and a floor per pixel rather than per Gaussian is pending.
  • Far, thin trims still become fat dashes in every cut. The bounds miss this error.
  • Not ported. The closed-shell back rule and the error cut live in athenea-pieces. The main engine’s CutSelector is unchanged.

7. Compression and the WebGPU player on the iPhone

The goal set on 10 October 2026 was the whole Corvette, with its full light transfer, drawn on an iPhone in a web page and judged against the mesh path trace. The first budget was 50–60 MB of resident data, and the small pieces had to look right. The player is a fork of Spark with a WebGPU backend (~/luc/sparkwebGPU, branch webgpu, session sparkwebgpu-ce); athenea writes the clouds, the merge trees, the pages and the occlusion. This section describes the formats, the streaming, the draw and the relight on the phone, and what was measured. Every figure below comes from that repository’s log (CLAUDE.md), its research notes (research/iphone-cost.md, research/iphone-compact.md, research/errcut.md), its format document (spark/docs/docs/athc-v3.md), its commit messages, athenea-pieces/docs/pieces.md §4 and athenea-renders/pieces/iphone/README.md.

Three kinds of measurement appear, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Dawn / M5 Pro: GPU timestamps from headless Dawn (Metal) on an Apple M5 Pro, at the phone’s CSS resolution (393 × 852) or at 1920 × 1080. They are a proxy for the phone’s GPU.
  • Emulated iPhone: the live site in desktop Chrome on the same M5 Pro, with the page in its mobile mode at 393 × 852. The pool sizes, the cut and the memory are the phone’s; the speed is not. Per pieces.md §4, the emulated figures run at about twice the real iPhone’s, so draw counts are judged on the device.
  • Real iPhone: iOS 18.7, Safari 26.6.1, a 393 × 852 @3 screen (the iPhone 14 Pro–16 class), canvas 393 × 695 at pixel ratio 1. These are the stress-page runs of 10–11 October and the user’s two tests of 12 October.

Image quality is given as PSNR in dB, or as relMSE (∑(x−y)2/∑y2\sum(x-y)^2/\sum y^2; where PSNR is written −10log⁡10relMSE-10\log_{10}\mathrm{relMSE} it is stated). The reference is either the path trace at athenea’s /World/Camera (1920 × 1080) or the player’s own uncompressed path. The reference used is named in each case.

7.1 The container: .athc version 3

athenea’s .athc v2 is a flat array of words per splat. The web player rewrites it as version 3, which is laid out by sections and tiers. Inside each block, the data a tier needs is a prefix, so each (block, tier) pair is one HTTP Range request:

Tier Sections Contents
1 CORE, SHRS, and optionally LODS, LODE, OCCL, LODM, TREE, SKIN splat geometry and colour; LoD metadata
2 MATL normals, emission, pbr, lobes
3 SHAD, CURV, TXDI, TXIN, TXFD open-direction bits, curvature, transfer (direct, indirect, reflected field)
4 CMPC the compact record for the phone (§7.4)

A browser offers gzip and nothing better (DecompressionStream), so the lossless lever is what gzip is given. Encoding 1 transposes each array into byte planes: byte 0 of every element, then byte 1, and so on. The high byte of an f16 or f32 changes little from one splat to the next in Morton order, so it forms runs that gzip finds. Encoding 2 does the same after replacing each 16-bit lane with its wrapped difference from the previous element. athc-convert --gzip --planes picks the smaller encoding per section. Measured on the published clouds: Corvette light 68.1 → 60.9 MB (−10.6 %), Corvette HD 288.7 → 248.6 MB (−13.9 %), its paint −23 %, pawn parts −17 to −33 %. The shadow bits are random, and they never gain.

7.2 Transfer compression by clustered PCA

The transfer is 30–70 % of a relit cloud’s bytes, and neighbouring transfers are close. Two clustered-PCA codings are used: a lossy encoding of the file (encoding 3), and a joint basis that the phone keeps resident.

7.2.1 Encoding 3 (file level)

Following Sloan et al. (2003), a transfer section of one block is stored as up to KK clusters (k-means on a sample; K≤n/128K \le n/128). Each cluster cc has a mean μc\mu_c and mc≤Mm_c \le M principal directions 𝒅c,j\mathbf{d}_{c,j} in f16. Each element ii has a cluster id and mcm_c quantised coefficients:

𝒕̂i=μc(i)+∑j<mc(i)(qijΔc(i))𝒅c(i),j,qij∈ℤ, \hat{\mathbf{t}}_i = \mu_{c(i)} + \sum_{j<m_{c(i)}} \left(q_{ij}\,\Delta_{c(i)}\right)\mathbf{d}_{c(i),j}, \qquad q_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z},

where every product and sum is rounded to f32 in that order and the result is rounded to the nearest half. This makes the Rust/WASM and TypeScript decoders bit-identical. The step Δc\Delta_c is proportional to the cluster’s RMS, which gives the same relative error in dim clusters as in bright ones. The coefficients are zig-zag integers of 1–3 bytes, stored as byte planes. mcm_c is the smallest count whose truncation takes at most 40 % of the error budget. The budget is relative per block:

Eblock=∑i‖𝒕̂i−𝒕i‖2∑i‖𝒕i‖2≤E,E=10−5by default, E_{\text{block}} = \frac{\sum_i \lVert \hat{\mathbf t}_i - \mathbf t_i\rVert^2}{\sum_i \lVert \mathbf t_i\rVert^2} \le E, \qquad E = 10^{-5}\ \text{by default},

and the step shrinks until the budget holds. Because the SH transfer coefficients are orthonormal, EE is the L2L^2 error over the sphere. A block that cannot meet EE, or that byte planes store smaller, keeps its values exactly. Measured: the transfer sections take 4–7× fewer bytes (the pawn top’s TX 112 went from 157 to 28 MB); a relit splat moves by relMSE 4×10−84\times10^{-8} to 3×10−53\times10^{-5}; the Corvette light set went from 67.9 to 49.5 MB and the pawn page from 281 to 84 MB.

7.2.2 The joint basis kept on the GPU

7.3 The open-direction bits by vector quantisation

Each gaussian’s 16 × 16 open-direction cells (256 bits) are replaced by an index into a dictionary (athc-convert --shadow-vq). The dictionary is k-means in Hamming space, each centroid the bitwise majority of its members with ties kept open, 12 rounds, seeded from member ⌊cn/C⌋\lfloor c\,n/C\rfloor. With 1024 centroids a piece, the image error is 62.9–65.4 dB (athenea’s figure: 62 dB), and the mean Hamming distance is 2.0–2.5 of 256 (p99 14–16). The raw masks as a dictionary would be 15.3 MB at 1M splats, against 1.51 MB for the VQ. Across six pieces, one asset dictionary of 4096 entries (131 KB, mean distance 1.07) beats six dictionaries of 1024 (197 KB, mean distance 1.00) on bytes for about the same error, so the shared dictionary is the default.

7.5 Error-band pages and the compact tier

7.5.1 The error cut

Each merged node nn carries the bounds εn\varepsilon_n (error) and ρn\rho_n (sphere radius) in f16 (LODE). With pp the pixels per unit at unit distance, the traversal refines nn when

Pn=εnpmax⁡(dn−ρn,near)>τ. P_n = \frac{\varepsilon_n\, p}{\max(d_n - \rho_n,\ \text{near})} > \tau .

τ\tau is in pixels. athenea pages her merge tree by error bands, as Nanite does. A node is needed exactly when its parent’s projected error exceeds τ\tau, so each node goes into the band (an octave) of its parent’s error. The bands are ordered coarse to fine, each in leaf order, and the order is cut into pages of P=3000P = 3000 nodes. A page-table row holds a box around the parents’ spheres and the largest and smallest parent error. A page is needed when

εpagemaxpmax⁡(dist⁡(eye,box),near)>τ(page 0 always). \frac{\varepsilon^{\max}_{\text{page}}\, p}{\max(\operatorname{dist}(\text{eye}, \text{box}),\ \text{near})} > \tau \quad(\text{page 0 always}).

Because the box bounds the parents’ spheres, this test is conservative against the traversal. On the t42 pieces, the settled paged cut was identical to the fully resident cut; residency was 2.27–2.53× the cut.

Two culls are applied in the traversal:

  • Back faces. A node of a closed part is pruned with its subtree when 𝒏⋅𝒗<−sin⁡(θ+arcsin⁡(ρ/d))\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v < -\sin(\theta + \arcsin(\rho/d)), with θ\theta the half-angle of its normal cone.
  • Occlusion. With the eye outside the asset’s box, a node is pruned when the four bilinear cells toward the eye (oct(𝒗)⋅16−0.5\mathrm{oct}(\mathbf v)\cdot16-0.5) are all closed in its occlusion-codebook entry.

athenea’s codebook for the car has 8192 entries (84 % of the ideal, with no false positives), and the v8 codebook has 8182. With quantised centres, occlusion is tested over the box of possible centres (split by orthant, where both octahedral coordinates are monotone). Over the car’s 10.7M nodes, at athenea’s cameras and 24 random eyes, this hid 0 nodes that the exact centre keeps, at a cost of at most 0.06 % of the hidden nodes being kept. With every cull on, the drawn count is −51 % against every cull off, at 36.9 dB between the two images.

7.5.4 Synthetic multi-level roots

A page-0 slot holds 3072 nodes, but athenea’s car5120 forests keep thousands of lone roots on purpose (merges never cross parts or her guards): fine5 has 29 213 and fine6 21 065. Past a fan-out (2048), the packer cuts the roots, in Morton order of their centres, into runs, each the children of a synthetic node, and repeats until page 0 can hold the rest. Each synthetic node has infinite error (always refined), a sphere around its children, no cone and no occlusion index, so the cut is the flat forest’s (tested at 5 eyes × 5 values of τ\tau). fine5 went from 10 files to 3 and fine6 from 8 to 1.

7.6 Streaming and the per-frame cut

The reader (CompactBandStreamer) reads only the page tables at open. After each traversal:

  1. The needed set comes from each tree’s page table alone, with the eye in the tree’s object space (the car’s turn from athenea’s Z-up stage) and foveated.
  2. The missing pages are requested in order of the error a page removes per byte it costs; ties go to the nearer page. Consecutive pages are merged into one Range request (up to 8 MiB), within the pool’s free room.
  3. The pool evicts pages that are not needed from the finest band down. Page 0 and any page a resident page depends on are never evicted.
  4. τ\tau rises until the needed set fits (tauToFit). The current version finds, by bisection, the lowest τ\tau that fits, which stopped the near-view oscillation (4.61–5.76 → 5.16).

A node whose children are absent is drawn as a leaf, or as its parent or children while a run is being decoded. With v4 runs, the cut’s own runs and those of its parents and children are always kept, and the rest are dropped first under a run budget. An extrapolated camera (2.5 updates ahead) is traversed too, so its runs are prefetched. Up to 12 Range requests are in flight.

7.7 The draw

The compact path (CompactRelitPath, compact.slang) runs five passes a frame:

  1. Project (one thread a cluster, only when the light changes): the 094 projections.
  2. Cull (one thread a listed node): frustum test; a 24-bit radial-distance key and the index appended with an atomic.
  3. Sort: GPU radix sort of the drawn keys, back to front (6 passes over 24 bits).
  4. Shade (one thread a drawn slot, binned by cluster): decode, relight (§7.8), project. A 16-byte record is written into the sort’s own buffers: centre as u16 at 1/16 px, σ1\sigma_1 (half) with the major-axis angle, σ2\sigma_2 (half) with α\alpha (half; a negative sign marks LoD opacity), and colour in RGB9E5.
  5. Draw: instanced quads, premultiplied over an rgba16float target.

Projection (EWA). With WW the view rotation, R,SR,S the splat’s rotation and scale, and JJ the perspective Jacobian at the view-space centre (x,y,z)(x,y,z):

J=(fx/z0−fxx/z20fy/z−fyy/z2),Σ2D=JWRS(JWRS)⊤=(abbd). J = \begin{pmatrix} f_x/z & 0 & -f_x x/z^2\\ 0 & f_y/z & -f_y y/z^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Sigma_{2D} = J\,W R S\,(J W R S)^{\!\top} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ b & d\end{pmatrix}.

Opacity compensation. The anti-aliasing blur adds β=0.3px2\beta = 0.3\ \text{px}^2 to the diagonal, and the opacity is scaled so the splat’s mass is kept:

Σ′=Σ2D+βI,α′=α0det⁡Σ2D/det⁡Σ′. \Sigma' = \Sigma_{2D} + \beta I,\qquad \alpha' = \alpha_0 \sqrt{\det\Sigma_{2D}/\det\Sigma'} .

A merged node with LoD opacity α0>1\alpha_0 > 1 is drawn with α0←min⁡(4α0−3,5)\alpha_0 \leftarrow \min(4\alpha_0 - 3, 5), and its quad is widened by 0.7(α0−1)0.7(\alpha_0-1) deviations. A faint splat (α′<4αmin\alpha' < 4\,\alpha_{\min}) keeps its mass α0det⁡Σ2D\alpha_0\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{2D}}: its blur is chosen so its opacity reaches the faint target, or it is drawn at that target with probability α′/(4αmin)\alpha'/(4\alpha_{\min}) by a stable hash dither. A thin glass sheet’s floor of 0.1 is applied after the blur and faded by its area on screen. Applied before the blur, it had made the headlight cover 1.63× too bright. The fragment evaluates α=α′e−z2/2\alpha = \alpha'\,e^{-z^2/2} inside the cut radius, discards under αmin\alpha_{\min}, and adds the per-pixel sharp lobes (§7.8).

A thread per drawn slot reuses the sort buffers as records, so nothing else is per-splat. The compact path has no relit records (84 B), no pixel records (48 B), no accumulator (32 B) and no ext splats (32 B). On the phone it also has no depth texture: nothing reads it. A still view re-runs only the draw. Writing directly to 8 bits saved no GPU time (Apple blends in tile memory) and changed the image by up to about 100 codes in 1–3 % of channels, so the target stays rgba16float.

7.8 Relighting on the phone

The shade evaluates athenea’s relitByDome (TX branch) for one dome. The code is a verbatim copy with the field reads taken from registers, because WebGPU cannot bind one buffer as both read and write in a dispatch. The kept terms come from the projections; the reflected field (16 coefficients × RGB) is read along the mirror. The following refinements were ported or added, and each was measured.

The reflected field. It is 42 % of the shade, and the cost is arithmetic, not memory. Two changes cut it: shading binned by cluster (a counting sort of the slots per cluster, with per-workgroup histograms), −6 to −9 %; and the field limited to degree 1 (4 coefficients) when a footprint is under 3 px, −23 % of the shade at 215k drawn. Against the path trace, the change in the door, sill, bumper and hood is under 0.01 dB.

Sharp per-pixel lobes. A coat or polish below the sharp roughness thresholds is not answered once at the centre. The shade subtracts the centre’s sky reading and hands the draw a 5-word record: the mirror direction at the centre (unorm16 × 2), its steps one pixel right and one pixel down (f16 pairs), the coat weight and both roughnesses as square roots, and the polish weight in RGB9E5. The fragment then reads the sky along oct−1(𝒂0+𝒔xδx+𝒔yδy)\mathrm{oct}^{-1}(\mathbf a_0 + \mathbf s_x\,\delta x + \mathbf s_y\,\delta y). The roughness read is built in three steps:

  • Unwidening. The bake widened the stored roughness by Toksvig, r′4=r4+2s2r'^4 = r^4 + 2s^2 with ss the normal spread. Since the per-pixel turn already accounts for that spread, the lobe reads r=max⁡(r′4−2s2,0)1/4r = \max(r'^4 - 2s^2, 0)^{1/4}.
  • Fold rule (athenea f9c9a2b). With t=2st = 2s the turn over ±2σ\pm2\sigma and w=smoothstep(0.7,1.5,t)w = \mathrm{smoothstep}(0.7, 1.5, t), the pixel’s turn is scaled by 1−w1-w and the lobe widens, α′=α2+w(t/2)2\alpha' = \sqrt{\alpha^2 + w\,(t/2)^2}, held under 0.5.
  • Pixel spread. The normal turns by 𝒅x,𝒅y\mathbf d_x,\mathbf d_y across a pixel. That box of slopes, doubled for the mirror, widens the GGX α=r2\alpha = r^2: α2←α2+2(‖𝒅x‖2+‖𝒅y‖2)12=α2+‖𝒅x‖2+‖𝒅y‖26. \alpha^2 \leftarrow \alpha^2 + \frac{2\left(\lVert\mathbf d_x\rVert^2+\lVert\mathbf d_y\rVert^2\right)}{12} = \alpha^2 + \frac{\lVert\mathbf d_x\rVert^2+\lVert\mathbf d_y\rVert^2}{6}.

9.2 The representation and the transfer

  • One normal, one footprint. A Gaussian has one normal and a cell’s footprint; thin curved strips and rolled edges are where this shows (§9.1). The coverage mapping assumes the cell lattice’s overlap 2πσ22\pi\sigma^2, which sub-cell triangles and edge-held Gaussians do not have (§3.12).
  • Glass. A tinted thin glass is converted solid, and a cloud keeps a single index of refraction unless it carries layers (§3.12). Thin-sheet Gaussians are lifted to a 0.1 opacity floor per Gaussian, so merging fewer, larger sheets varies the transmission cell to cell; a floor per pixel is pending (§6.11).
  • Rank-one field. The reflected field’s coupling to the sky puts a lit ground’s colour, not its shape, into a reflection under a sky lit from one side; at degree 3 it resolves a horizon to about twenty degrees (correlation 0.56 at degree 3, 0.63 at degree 5, 0.85 with a 32 × 32 octahedral map, in simulation) (§4.11).
  • Lights inside the scene. One sun is extracted per dome. Lamps inside a scene are shadowed by bits that say which directions leave the scene, not which reach the lamp (§4.11).
  • Size on device. Device memory bounds a part at about 5 M leaves for the merge tree; the 4.5 M cap made larger parts convert at a coarser camera resolution (Bottom at 3392 px, Part_3 and Front_Bumper_001 at 4480 px; §3.11, §6.9).
  • Instancing. Nested point instancers are expanded at the innermost level only, and motion authored as velocities is read at the sample (§3.12).

9.3 Animated assets

  • Four influences per Gaussian truncate the sparrow’s wing tips (27 % of wing vertices carry more); the share of truncated Gaussians is unmeasured (§5.3, §5.13).
  • No dual-quaternion skinning for clouds, no retargeting and no clip blending; several skinned meshes at different prim transforms keep only the first one’s (§5.13).
  • The traced route poses once per frame, so it does not blur a skinned cloud, and LoD instances carry no motion (§5.13).
  • Per-part visibility leaves out a part’s own self-shadowing, assumes rigid parts that occlude independently, and has not been baked beyond about 3 M Gaussians per part; zonal transfers keep only the direct half and the bind pose’s self-shadowing (§4.11, §5.8, §5.13).
  • The zonal fit’s error on the sparrow, and a geometric measure of coverage at a close bend, are unmeasured (§5.13).

9.4 Levels of detail, streaming and the player

  • Residency. Pages hold about 4× the cut because needed and view-culled nodes share every page; the phone splits traversal from payload rather than shrinking pages (§6.8, §6.11).
  • Occlusion weighting. The shared codebook is apportioned by entries used, not by how often a view culls a node; weighting by an orbit of views in the bake should lift culling past 84 % of the ideal (§6.11).
  • Merge opacity on the phone. The player draws each merge’s mass, while athenea’s rasteriser keeps the least-squares coverage; the coverage fit is the better choice except on the grille (§6.4).
  • The cut on the phone. The compact traversal does not keep a merge’s LoD size, so a combined size/error cut has nothing to guard with, and the error cut alone did not beat the size cut in every view (§7.13).
  • Not ported. The closed-shell back rule and the error cut live in athenea-pieces; the main engine’s CutSelector is unchanged (§6.11).

9.5 The engine

  • Splats are not yet inside the path tracer’s secondary rays: with meshes, splats are composited over path-traced surfaces by depth and cast shadows onto them, but a bounce ray does not see them (§2.16).
  • The rasteriser’s motion and lens blur are moment-matched Gaussians, up to 38 % too peaked for long streaks (§2.16).
  • Gaussians a few hundredths of a pixel across vanish from the raster; levels of detail are the remedy (§2.16).
  • Raster mesh visibility pays about 8 ms of host time per 1800 draws; material compilation is synchronous; the TLAS is rebuilt every frame; lens distortion beyond k1,k2k_1, k_2 is not applied (§2.16).

9.6 Gaps in the validation

  • v8 on the phone. The 5120 px close-up level’s trees and shared occlusion are written (§6.9), but its conversion for the player was in progress when this was written. Its quality against the path trace, its download and its behaviour on the real iPhone are unmeasured (§7.13). In particular, the close-up softness that motivated it (§7.12.1) has been shown resolved only on a logo-and-grille test (§3.11), not on the whole car on the device.
  • The 512-cluster basis has not been measured on the real phone (§7.2.2).
  • Real-device timing exists only for the stress ramps and the user’s two tests (§7.13); the emulated phone’s speed is not the phone’s.
  • One production asset. The car-level figures are for one asset, the Corvette, with the sparrow and the pawn as the animated and rigid controls. Generality to other assets has not been measured.
  • Shared machine. Bake and tree timings were taken on a machine shared with other GPU jobs and are not benchmarks (§3.11, §8.9).

9.7 Future work

In the order the measurements suggest:

  1. Measure v8 against the path trace and on the real iPhone, close up and far, with the 512-cluster basis.
  2. Bake the lamps’ emission into the transfer, so that the headlights and their light on the car match the path trace.
  3. Fit curvature over each Gaussian’s footprint from mesh samples, to halve the hood highlights’ offset and steady the per-pixel turn.
  4. Move to 32 × 32 open-direction cells where a sharp coat mirrors the car itself (the door’s open class).
  5. Address thin chrome tubes and rolled lips (the bumper’s strip and lower lip), the rim’s near-ground reflection and far thin trims.
  6. Weight the occlusion codebook by an orbit of views, and port the error cut and the closed-shell rule into the main engine’s CutSelector.
  7. For animated assets: more than four influences, cross-part occlusion in poses other than the bind pose, and visibility bakes beyond 3 M Gaussians per part.
